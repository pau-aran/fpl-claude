## 4. Findings

### 4.1 [PROCESS — NEW RULE] A doubt you can bench is not a doubt you sell

Bruno G. was priced **0.60** — a genuine, well-sourced doubt (four games missed, Howe on
6 Feb: "getting closer… trained Wednesday… fingers crossed", a late call). The overlay was
*not* wrong; 0.60 was the right number on the evidence. **The error was converting it into
a transfer.** He started, played 90, and returned **11**.

The asymmetry we missed: **benching is reversible and free, selling is neither.** We held
**4 free transfers** and a bench slot. Benching him would have cost nothing and preserved
the option; selling him spent an FT, paid the spread, and turned a 40% downside into a
permanent zero. The GW24 memo had actually written the right instinct — *"doubt+fixture
argue bench-first, decide-next-week"* — and then we did the opposite one week later.

**RULE (GW26+): a player priced 0.4–0.7 is a BENCH decision, not a SELL decision, whenever
we hold a spare FT and a bench slot. Sell a doubt only when it is priced below ~0.3, when
the absence is duration-scoped past this GW, or when the FT is needed elsewhere and the
slot is genuinely dead.** Selling into uncertainty forecloses the option we are paying to
keep.

*Forward note, recorded for the audit trail and NOT used to regrade:* later gameweeks will
judge this sale far more kindly than this week does. That is irrelevant to the grade — the
decision was wrong on the information available, and being rescued by an injury we could
not see is not a process credit.

### 4.2 [VARIANCE — the new bench-hair lever's first outing cost 5]

The mechanical hair listing shipped this week and worked exactly as designed: it surfaced
**two sub-0.2 margins**, including the Tarkowski/Calvert-Lewin 0.02 that the old checklist
would very likely have missed — that is the GW22 failure mode, where the hair nobody named
cost 8 points. We named it, called it, and **lost 5** (Tarkowski 2, C-L 7 benched).

Grading this a PASS, deliberately. The reasoning was the accumulated evidence — two prior
stings (Konaté GW19 0.06 → 7; **Tarkowski GW22 0.14 → 8**) both in the shape "benched
defender hauls" — and a 0.02 projected gap is a coin-flip by construction. Losing a
coin-flip is not a process defect. **But the honest lesson is that the lever is not free:
the listing guarantees the hair is SEEN, it does not tell us which way to call it.** The
[WATCH] on widening the defender-clean-sheet band to ~0.3 now has evidence pointing *both*
ways (two hauls when benched, one blank when started) and stays open.

### 4.3 [WATCH] The no-rebuy rule cost 5 this week

Banning Szoboszlai (sold GW24) sent the slot to Casemiro: **8 v 3**. The rule is sound —
it exists to stop flip-flopping that pays spread twice — but this is the first time it has
had a visible price. One data point; the rule stands. Logged to see whether it recurs.

## 5. New rules from this week

1. **[PROCESS] Bench a doubt, don't sell it** (§4.1) — 0.4–0.7 priced players are bench
   decisions while a spare FT and a bench slot exist.
2. **[PROCESS] A captaincy switch needs a margin, not just a direction.** GW25's switch
   was justified on an availability flag but executed on a **+0.30 xPts** edge, which is
   inside noise. **From GW26: deviating from the shield requires the availability flag AND
   a board margin of ≥0.5 xPts on the single-GW column** — otherwise hold the shield and
   let the flag express itself through the vice.
