# GW26 review — the chip fired on the wrong Arsenal player

## 1. Score summary — predicted 85.55 (= base XI 55.85 + captain 29.70 tripled), actual 56 (−2 vs avg 58; roll, TRIPLE CAPTAIN played)

**56 vs 58. The first chip of the season lost two points to the field.** Season 1605… no:
**1499**, edge +194 (from +196). The chip did not fail because the double failed — Arsenal
played both legs and our three doublers logged 180 minutes each. It failed on *which* of
them wore the armband.

| tripled | actual | ×3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Gabriel (chosen)** | 7 | **21** |
| Rice | **14** | **42** |
| J.Timber | 3 | 9 |

**Tripling Rice instead would have scored 77, not 56.** The decision cost **−21**, which is
the single largest decision cost of the season.

## 2. The decision — graded

**PROCESS: PARTIAL FAIL. Not because the reasoning was absent, but because the memo
identified the exact failure mode and took the bet anyway on a 0.99 xPts edge.**

The GW26 memo says, in its own risk paragraph: *"tripling a centre-back is a bet on two
clean sheets, and clean sheets are bimodal. Both Arsenal legs are away, and if Arsenal
concede in both, a tripled defender can return 6 where a tripled forward returns 20."*
Arsenal drew both legs, conceded in both, and Gabriel returned 7. **The risk was named,
priced qualitatively, and then overridden by a mean-projection difference of 0.99.**

That is the lesson, and it generalises well past this chip:

> **[NEW RULE — multiplier chips] For a chip that multiplies ONE player, rank candidates by
> the shape of their return distribution, not only by mean xPts. A defender's projection is
> concentrated in a step function (clean sheet or not); a midfielder's or forward's is
> continuous (goals, assists, bonus, and a clean sheet worth 1 not 4). When the mean margin
> between a defender and an attacking asset is under ~1.5 xPts, the multiplier goes on the
> attacker.** Rice was 0.99 behind Gabriel on the mean and far ahead on ceiling; the rule
> would have picked him.

The rest of the week grades clean:
- **ROLL — PASS.** Refused the three-move bundle (Konaté → Guéhi, Casemiro → Semenyo,
  Calvert-Lewin → Kroupi). All three refusals stand: two of the buys blank in GW31.
- **Vice Rice over Timber — PASS**, and ironically correct about which player to trust.
- **Bench Boost held — PASS.** No bench slot doubled; the rule held.

## 3. Calibration log (split format)

| GW | predicted | = base XI | + captain | actual | base err | cap err |

The largest captain-slot error of the season, and it is structural rather than noise: a
tripled slot multiplies the variance by three as well as the mean. **The split format
earned its keep here** — read as a single number, 85.55 → 56 looks like a model failure;
split, it is a base XI 8.85 under and a tripled captain 20.70 under, which localises the
loss precisely to the chip decision.

## 4. Findings

1. **[NEW RULE] Multiplier chips are a variance decision** (§2). Rank on distribution
   shape, not mean, when the margin is under ~1.5.
2. **[CONFIRMED] The point-in-time gate was load-bearing.** Before it, the advisor wanted
   the TC held for a GW33 double built on a rearrangement not yet made. The chip would have
   been *saved on hindsight* — a worse failure than playing it on the wrong player, because
   it would have been invisible.
3. **[WATCH] Predicted 85.55 was never realistic.** A tripled DGW defender's projection
   sums two clean-sheet expectations and then triples them, compounding an optimistic
   assumption three ways. Watch whether DGW predictions run high generally.
