## Manager overlay (the human call, outside the algorithm)

**The week we buy the double and break the shield.**

**Transfers (2 of 4 FT, no hit, 2 banked): Saliba → Gabriel, Bruno G. → Casemiro.**

The solver's unconstrained proposal was a four-move bundle (adding Konaté → Guéhi and Calvert-Lewin → João Pedro) worth +6.75. Two of the four are refused and the refusals are the point:

- **Konaté LOCKED.** The case for selling him is one fixture — Liverpool host Man City, our worst clean-sheet spot of the week (2.65 xPts). Selling a nailed defender on a single hard fixture is the thing the plan explicitly forbids: we buy runs, not single gameweeks. His run after this is SUN(a)3, NFO(a)3, WHU(h)2, WOL(a)2. And the proposed replacement, Guéhi, is a Man City asset who **blanks in BGW31**, which is the one structural fact we already know about the run-in.
- **Calvert-Lewin LOCKED.** He was bought at GW24, one week ago, as the BGW31-playing bench forward. Selling him now for João Pedro would pay the spread twice on a piece whose whole purpose is six weeks away, and would hand back a blank-proof slot. Never re-buy what you just sold, and never re-sell what you just bought, absent new information. There is none.
- **Szoboszlai BANNED.** With Konaté and Calvert-Lewin locked, the solver's next idea was to buy Szoboszlai back — sold at GW24 as the funding leg for B.Fernandes, one week ago, at a price that has since fallen £0.3m. That is the re-buy rule exactly. Banned, and Casemiro (£5.6m, Man Utd) takes the slot £1.1m cheaper.

**Why Saliba → Gabriel, when Saliba has been locked all season.** This looks like the eleven-times-vetoed Arsenal-defender churn and it is not, so the distinction has to be written down. Every prior veto refused a *sideways* swap between two fit defenders at materially equal projection. This one is a funded upgrade to the single best asset in the game for the gameweek that is four days away: **DGW26 is confirmed (Arsenal play Brentford away and Wolves away), and on our own board Gabriel projects 9.90 across the double against Saliba's 7.34, Timber's 9.10 and Rice's 8.91.** He is also better this week (5.26 v 3.90). That is +1.36 now and +2.56 next week, and it upgrades the triple-captain target we have been holding the Arsenal triple for since GW17. The 3-per-club cap is what forced the choice: we cannot own Gabriel and Saliba both, so the double had to be bought by selling into it.

**Why Bruno G. is the funding sale.** He is priced 0.60 — four games missed, Howe (6 Feb) "getting closer… trained Wednesday… fingers crossed", a late call. That is a genuine doubt with the fixture flipping favourably (Brentford at home), so this is not a distress sale; it is that he is our weakest midfielder either way (2.77 this week, 4.00 next, and he does not double), and the standing plan named him a live sell candidate the moment he could not be trusted. Selling him funds Gabriel's £1.1m premium and still leaves the bank healthier than it started (£0.7m → £1.1m).

**CAPTAIN: B.Fernandes, not Haaland. This is the first captaincy deviation of the season and it ends a 24/24 run — it needs more justification than it needs enthusiasm.** The shield rule forbids recency-driven switches, and a form slump alone would not move me. Three things that are not recency do:

1. **Availability.** Haaland is priced 0.80. He was *benched for the Carabao Cup semi-final on Wed 4 Feb* — read as a rest before Anfield — and Guardiola at the Friday presser said "I don't know, we'll decide tomorrow." That is a datable availability flag, which is the shield rule's own written carve-out.
2. **Our board flipped.** B.Fernandes projects **5.54 to Haaland's 5.24**. This is the first week all season our own numbers have preferred someone else, which is the exact condition GW24's memo set for a defensible switch ("if the board's own numbers flip decisively").
3. **The switch is free.** We already own B.Fernandes, so unlike every previous temptation this costs no transfer and no EO gamble — the community's captaincy poll has him above 50% and Haaland fourth, so this is the low-variance side, not a differential punt.

The honest counter-argument, recorded so the review can grade it: **the margin is only +0.30 xPts (+0.60 doubled), which is thin**, and at GW23 we priced Haaland at 0.70 on a similar flag, captained him anyway, and he played 17 minutes. That precedent says we have already paid once for over-adherence. Vice **Gabriel** (Arsenal at home to Sunderland, FDR2) rather than Haaland, so a Haaland scratch does not cascade.

**Bench hairs — both sub-0.2 margins named and called** (the new mechanical listing, first outing):

- **0.02 — Dúbravka (Burnley, West Ham H, FDR2) starts over Roefs (Sunderland, Arsenal A, FDR5).** Accepting the solver's order, but on the duel and not on the hair: our £5.0m first-choice keeper is away at the league leaders and the £4.0m bench keeper is at home to a bottom side. Fixture decides a coin-flip.
- **0.02 — Tarkowski FORCED into the XI over Calvert-Lewin.** This is the [WATCH] case that has now cost us twice, and both times in this exact shape: a bimodal defender clean-sheet bet benched on a hair (Konaté GW19, 0.06, returned 7; **Tarkowski GW22, 0.14, returned 8**). The projected gap between a defender and a forward understates the realised spread, because a clean sheet is a step function the model smooths. Tarkowski is a nailed 90-minute centre-back at Fulham; Calvert-Lewin is priced 0.80 with a thinner floor. Pulling `force_start` — the lever the GW19 review said existed and was not used.
