## Headline: Haaland goes AWAY — the gap narrows but he's still the pick

GW9 was the first "normal" round after the big GW8 wildcard week, so the market
arrived largely settled on the post-break template. The defining captaincy
wrinkle: **Haaland was away at Aston Villa (MCI at AVL, A)** — his first genuinely
awkward captain fixture in weeks after a run of home/soft games (he came off a
GW8 home double v Everton and 11 goals in 8 league games). That single fixture did
what nothing else had since GW7: it let the field **close the gap** on him in the
polls. But the crowd did not blink — Villa's own defensive numbers (mid-table for
xGC, eight conceded in eight) plus Haaland's absurd baseline kept him the clear
consensus captain. The other headline stories were continuity: **Woltemade's
bandwagon rolling on**, **Salah's exodus accelerating** (now the season's
second-most-sold behind the injured Palmer), and **Palmer still out** and still
bleeding owners.

**Consensus: Haaland captain — still the pick, but for the first time this season
a real second option (Semenyo, home v Forest) was in the frame.** The away trip to
Villa narrowed the FFScout margin from GW8's ~71% and lifted Semenyo into a
genuine #2, yet the elite EO (~90%+ top-10k) shows the crowd stuck with Haaland.
Captaining Haaland was the safe, no-EO-risk move; captaining Semenyo was the
defensible-but-contrarian play, not consensus.

## 2. Transfer market of the week (pre-deadline)

Per Fantasy Football Fix "FPL Gameweek 9 Transfer Trends: Salah Sold, Woltemade
Surges", allaboutfpl's GW9 pieces, FFScout's "Buy, keep or sell? … ahead of
Gameweek 9" (2025-10-21) and premierleague.com's Scout GW9 transfer radar
(news/4444935, pre-deadline, "ahead of Friday's 18:30 BST deadline"):

**In — the post-wildcard template deepens:**
- **Woltemade — most-bought again, ~550,000 in.** The bandwagon that went live in
  GW8 kept rolling; four goals in five Newcastle starts, on the scoresheet in three
  straight, and a 6.4 ppm second only to Haaland among forwards. The single
  clearest "everyone is buying this" signal of the week.
- **Saka — still a heavy buy**, continuing the GW8 wave into Arsenal's soft run
  (CRY H, BUR A, SUN A, then the NLD); a near-template mid whose ownership kept
  climbing.
- **Mateta — a fresh bandwagon** off a 17-point GW8 haul (3 goals, 3 bonus v
  Bournemouth); named among the fastest risers.
- **Gakpo — the "Salah alternative" of choice** for managers keeping Liverpool
  exposure: 21 shots (most of any mid over GW1-8), joint-2nd for chances created
  (17), best non-penalty xGI among mids (4.18) per the Scout radar. The route into
  Liverpool that *wasn't* Salah.

**Out:**
- **Salah — most-transferred-out of the week**, and the season's second-most-sold
  overall behind only the injured Palmer. Six points in his last three despite a
  strong underlying xFPL; the away trip to Brentford (A) did nothing to stem the
  exodus, and wildcarders/rollers kept ditching him.
- **Isak — sold in droves** (the Newcastle attack budget flowing to Woltemade
  instead), continuing the GW8 "don't chase Isak's form slump" read.
- **Palmer — still out, still selling.** No return; the GW8 six-week news
  (Maresca's "I was wrong" presser) kept a steady sell wave running for anyone who
  hadn't yet cashed out.

## 3. Strategy roadmaps in circulation that week

- **Post-wildcard settling, not another mass-chip week.** Unlike GW8, GW9 was a
  hold-and-tinker round: most had banked wildcards, so the content shifted to
  fixture-run positioning and single-transfer buy/sell (FFScout "Buy, keep or
  sell?", 2025-10-21; allaboutfpl "two to buy/hold/sell").
- **Fixture-run targets (GW9-12+):** **Arsenal** the marquee run — CRY (H), BUR
  (A), SUN (A) then the North London Derby; 5th for attacking FDR — driving the
  Saka/Trossard/Gabriel buys. **Man United** flagged as fixtures turning (MUN v
  BHA, H — Brighton had one clean sheet in eight, 12 conceded), with **Sesko**
  (7 SoT, 12 shots in the box, 5 big chances) the differential route and
  Mbeumo/B.Fernandes the owned ones. **Brighton** topped the attacking FDR for
  GW10-15 as the *next* run to pre-empt (Minteh the standout, 33 pts in 8).
  **Bournemouth** (Semenyo captaincy backing, home v Forest) still a green pick.
- **Differentials gaining traction (sub-10% owned):** **Trossard** ("best-value
  route into Arsenal's attack" for those priced out of Saka), **Minteh** (Brighton),
  **Sesko** (Man United) — the rotowire/Fix GW9 differential lists.
- **Template reveal — FPL General's GW9 (FFScout, 2025-10-24):** still fully
  committed to his **no-Salah, no-Haaland** structure; **captained Saka**, swapped
  keepers to avoid an Arsenal triple-up, held João Pedro "one more week" despite
  wanting to sell, and backed Gyökeres to kick on after a midweek (UCL) brace.
  (FLAGGED: the "Saka 14-pointer" result quoted in the piece is post-deadline.)

## 4. Effective-ownership context for OUR holds

- **Haaland (we hold, captain — away at Villa):** the one week the "is he still the
  automatic captain?" question was legitimately live. Verdict from the market:
  **yes, still captain him.** Elite EO on the armband stayed ~90%+ (top-10k ~95%),
  the FFScout poll kept him #1 "by a distance", and Villa's defence is beatable.
  Captaining Haaland remains the zero-EO-risk move; the *only* defensible
  alternative is **Semenyo at home to Forest** (our own hold — see below), which is
  a contrarian differential, not the consensus. Captain Haaland; log Semenyo as the
  live-but-not-required hedge.
- **B. Fernandes + Mbeumo (we hold — MUN v Brighton, H):** the market read this as
  a genuinely **green fixture** (Brighton: one clean sheet in eight, 12 conceded).
  Owning both United attackers on that fixture is EO-positive; **Mbeumo** in
  particular was a rising transfer-in name and Man United's fixtures were flagged as
  turning under Amorim. No pressure to move either — this is a hold-and-benefit
  week. Sesko was the differential the crowd eyed *into* United, but we already have
  the owned routes.
- **Semenyo + Senesi (we hold — BOU v Forest, H):** **Semenyo is maximally
  template and the week's #2 captain** — most-owned and top-scoring mid, 42 of his
  70 pts at home, 10+ ppm at the Vitality. Holding him is EO-safe and he is our own
  ready-made captain hedge if we wanted to fade the Haaland away trip. **Senesi**
  (Bournemouth defender) carries clean-sheet + attacking upside on a home game vs
  Forest and remained a popular value pick. Both strong holds.
- **Brooks (we hold — injured, benched):** irrelevant to EO this week; he sits on
  the bench either way. Bournemouth's home fixture is moot for a non-starter — no
  action, no EO exposure.
- **Ekitiké (we hold — LIV at Brentford, A):** the awkward one. Liverpool are
  wobbling, **Salah is the most-sold player in the game**, and the Scout's endorsed
  Liverpool route was **Gakpo, not the forwards**. Ekitiké is low-EO with an away
  trip — no crowd pressure to captain or sell him, but also no tailwind. Hold; a
  Liverpool haul here would come through Gakpo/Salah more than Ekitiké, so limited
  EO downside if we do nothing. With **1 FT and ~£0.1m bank**, there's no affordable
  upgrade that beats holding this week regardless.
