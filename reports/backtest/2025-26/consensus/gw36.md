## Headline: the season's smallest double (two clubs) but the most motivated — City are 5 points behind with the game in hand, and that game in hand IS leg 2; Palace, by contrast, played a European semi-final 48 hours before our deadline and have a final to protect

**The double is real but it is narrow.** Only Man City and Crystal Palace play twice, against six
clubs in DGW33. The upside is concentrated: City host **Brentford** (Sat 9 May 16:30) and then
**Palace** (Wed 13 May 19:00) — **two home games** — while Palace host Everton before travelling to
the Etihad. premierleague.com's official **DGW36 Scout Selection** takes a **City triple-up of
Haaland (£14.6m), Rayan Cherki (£6.6m) and Nico O'Reilly (£5.2m)** and enters Palace **through the
defence only — Dean Henderson (£5.1m) and Maxence Lacroix (£5.2m) — explicitly "due to heavy
rotation in attack recently."** That one sentence is the most useful Palace datum of the week.

**The two clubs' motivation could not be more different.** After MW35 (Mon 4 May, City drew 3-3 at
Everton) **Arsenal lead on 76 points from 35 played; City are second on 71 from 34 — and the game
in hand is this very rearranged Palace fixture.** City must win both legs to keep a title race
alive. The counterweight is equally datable: **City are in the FA Cup final against Chelsea at
Wembley on Sat 16 May — three days after leg 2** — confirmed by the same 28-29 April fixture
amendment batch that moved **Bournemouth v Man City and Chelsea v Spurs from Sun 17 May to Tue 19
May**. Palace's situation is the mirror image: mid-table and safe, **nothing left in the league**,
having played a **UEFA Conference League semi-final second leg on Thursday 7 May** (48h before our
deadline) with a **European final on 27 May** waiting. A club with a trophy to win in a competition
and nothing to win in the league rotates the league games. **Buy the City side of this double, not
the Palace side — and buy Palace only through the two defenders the Scout named.**

## 4. Chip watch — the smallest double of the season, and we hold three chips

- **Our inventory: Wildcard, Free Hit and Bench Boost all banked. Triple Captain SPENT (GW26).**
  This is the last double of the season and the last structural chip trigger; GW37 and GW38 are
  ordinary rounds with no blanks or doubles.
- **What the community actually planned, with dates:**
  - **FFScout, 9 April 2026 — "The ultimate FPL chip strategy guide – for all 16 scenarios!"**
    offered two canonical run-in paths: **(a) WC32 → BB33 → FH34** or **(b) FH33 → WC35 → BB36**.
    So a **Bench Boost at GW36** was on the community's map from early April, *conditional* on a
    City double materialising ("there's no doubt that Erling Haaland will be a great Triple Captain
    option **should Man City get a Double Gameweek**").
  - **FFScout, 28-29 April 2026** — with DGW36 confirmed, the consensus hardened: *"Double Gameweek
    36 has officially been announced for Manchester City and Crystal Palace, so those active
    managers with chips to use will be playing a **Gameweek 35 Wildcard** before backing it up with
    a **Gameweek 36 Bench Boost**."* FFScout ran a **29 April** counter-piece — **"Is THIS a better
    option than GW35 Wildcard + GW36 Bench Boost?"** — which is itself evidence that WC35 → BB36
    was the default line being argued against, not a fringe view.
  - **Those who had already Bench Boosted at DGW33** were pointed at a **Free Hit** instead.
  - **premierleague.com, GW35** ran "The Scout's Wildcard squad for Gameweek 35" (4642477) — the
    official site walking managers through exactly that WC35 setup for the DGW36 payoff.
- **The honest size comparison.** DGW33 had **six** doubling clubs (Leeds, Bournemouth, Brighton,
  Chelsea, Burnley, Man City) and was universally called the season's biggest. **DGW36 has two.**
  With the 3-per-club cap, the theoretical maximum is **6 doubling players** out of 15 — against
  10-12 achievable in DGW33. A chip aimed here is aimed at a materially smaller target, and the
  community's framing reflects that: GW36 is "**the last hurrah for those still holding a chip**",
  i.e. the best remaining option rather than a great one.
- **BUT — the counter-argument that matters for a BENCH BOOST specifically:** **all 20 clubs play
  in GW36**, so **every one of the 15 has a fixture** and a boosted bench has **no dead men**. That
  is not true of most doubles (DGW33 sat next to BGW34). A Bench Boost here is "15 players × at
  least one game, up to 6 of them × two" — which is a perfectly respectable, low-variance version
  of the chip even though the double is narrow. The binding constraint is bench QUALITY, not
  fixtures, which is exactly what a preceding Wildcard is for.
- **What that means for us:**
  1. **Triple Captain is unavailable** — the single best use of DGW36 (Haaland, two home must-win
     games) is closed to us. Do not manufacture a substitute for it.
  2. **Bench Boost is the live chip.** Every bench slot plays. Its value is bounded by how many
     starting-quality bodies we can field, not by the size of the double.
  3. **A Wildcard is only right if we independently need 4+ permanent changes** (the standing
     `optimize/chip_timing.py` / knowledge.md rule). With three ordinary gameweeks left after this
     one, a Wildcard's payback horizon is short — the community's WC35 was played the week *before*
     the double precisely so its purchases could double; a WC at GW36 itself buys three weeks of
     benefit.
  4. **Free Hit has no blank left to answer.** There are no more blank gameweeks this season. A
     Free Hit at GW36 is purely a "field six doubling assets for one week" play, which the 3-per-club
     cap limits to two clubs' worth — a weaker version of what a Free Hit is for.
  5. **The gate:** DGW36 was **not confirmed until 28-29 April** (see below). A chip aimed at GW36
     is legitimate point-in-time **from GW35 onwards**, and NOT before.
