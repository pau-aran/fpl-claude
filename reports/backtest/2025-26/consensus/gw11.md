## Headline: the "hard" captain fixture that everyone captained anyway

GW11 handed the template a paradox. Haaland's fixture was **MCI v LIV (H)** — on
paper a top-two clash, the single hardest-looking captain fixture of his season so
far. Normally that reopens the armband debate. Instead the crowd stayed on Haaland
almost unmoved, because **Liverpool arrived in free-fall — five losses in their
last six league games**, a leaking defence (home defeats to PSV 1-4 and Nottingham
Forest 0-3 fresh in the memory), and eight points already off the Arsenal pace. A
"hard" fixture against a broken defence is a soft fixture. Haaland (13 goals in 10)
stayed the poll leader; the only serious alternatives were the **Chelsea assets v
Wolves (H)** — Wolves rock-bottom and the standout fixture on the board — and the
in-form **Bruno Fernandes / Bukayo Saka**.

The dominant *transfer* story was the mirror image: **the Liverpool exodus**. Salah
(4 goals in 12, and travelling to the Etihad) and the Liverpool defence were the
most-sold bloc in the game, with the widely-shared strategist line being **"don't
buy Liverpool or Man Utd back until GW12"** — i.e. sit out one more week of
Liverpool's bad run before their fixtures ease. That matters directly to us: we own
**Konaté + Szoboszlai**, both away at City this week, both inside exactly the run
the community was telling managers to wait out.

- **Haaland (C)** — ~90% EO among the top ranks; captaining him is the shield, not a
  bet. Correct.
- **Mbeumo, B.Fernandes** — both heavily-owned template, both nailed and in form;
  we are on the right side of the crowd. Hold.
- **Mateta** — still a popular hold/buy; template. Hold.
- **Konaté + Szoboszlai (the soft spot)** — the crowd was actively SELLING this
  Liverpool bloc, and the away trip to City is the worst week of their run. Holding

  i.e. the run turns after this one week (NFO(H), WHU(A), SUN(H)). Selling a
  just-bought Konaté (GW10) into one bad fixture, then watching his run turn green,
  is the churn the plan forbids. **Hold through the one week; do not chase the sell.**
- **Semenyo, Senesi (Bournemouth at Villa, A)** — no template pressure; a mid-tier
  fixture, held on value.
- **Saliba** — the market wasn't moving him; the only pressure on Saliba is our *own*
  optimizer's recurring Saliba→Timber churn proposal (see decision memo), not the

## 4. Where we diverge from the template (and why it's defensible)

1. **Holding the Liverpool bloc the crowd is selling.** Reasoning: it's one bad
   fixture inside a run the same strategists say to buy back into at GW12; Konaté was
   a GW10 buy (no new info to justify re-selling); the sale would spend our only FT
   on churn. EO risk logged and accepted.
2. **Rolling the FT rather than taking the fixture-led Chelsea/Enzo move.** We have no
   Chelsea route that isn't a −4, and a −4 for a one-week Wolves fixture fails the EV
   gate and the "two spending FTs > one FT + a −4" rule. Bank the FT.
