## Headline: Haaland comes HOME — the debate reopens, the answer stays the same

After the one-week away wobble at Villa (a GW9 blank), **Haaland was back home to
Bournemouth (MCI v BOU, H)** — a plum bounce-back fixture. That would normally end
all captaincy discussion, but two things kept GW10 "more nuanced than usual"
(FFScout's framing): Haaland was coming off a rare blank, and **Arsenal had the
softest fixture on the board (BUR, A)** with Saka and Gabriel both live. The
result: Haaland stayed the FFScout poll leader but "by a smaller margin than in
recent weeks", with the Arsenal duo the named alternatives. On the transfer side
the week had one dominant story — **the João Pedro → Mateta migration**, the most
popular single move in the game by a distance, as **Crystal Palace opened a
favourable six-game run** and the crowd piled into the in-form, cheaper Palace
striker. Alongside it, **Mbeumo was the single most-bought player** (our hold),
and **Salah's slow-motion exodus continued** as he lost his guaranteed start.

**Consensus: Haaland (C) — home to Bournemouth, the default bounce-back armband.**
The GW9 away blank plus Arsenal's Burnley fixture reopened the debate and narrowed
the FFScout margin, lifting **Saka (and Gabriel) to genuine alternative status**,
but the crowd did not switch — Haaland at home is the zero-EO-risk pick. Captaining
Saka was the defensible fade for anyone wanting Arsenal-double insurance; it was an
alternative, not the consensus.

## 2. Transfer market of the week (pre-deadline)

Per Fantasy Football Fix "FPL Gameweek 10 Transfers: Mateta, Mbeumo & Sarr Lead",
FFScout's "Buy, keep or sell? … ahead of Gameweek 10" (2025-10-28), premierleague's
Scout GW10 transfer radar (news/4450200, "Saturday's 13:30 GMT deadline") and
allaboutfpl's GW10 transfer pieces:

**In — the Palace run opens and the United bandwagon peaks:**
- **Mbeumo — the single most-bought player of the week, ~961,000 in.** Manchester
  United's fixtures had turned and the Mbeumo wave (already rolling since GW8/9)
  hit its crest ahead of MUN at Forest (A). This is *our hold* — maximally
  EO-positive.
- **Mateta — 2nd most-bought, ~547,000 in, and the week's defining pick.**
  **João Pedro → Mateta was the most popular single transfer in the game (~37.7%),
  by a distance.** Palace (£7.6–7.7m) opened a favourable six-game run; Mateta had
  4 goals in his last 6, elite underlying numbers (**29 shots, 2nd only to Haaland's
  37 among forwards**), and was £1.3m cheaper than João Pedro. premierleague ran a
  dedicated "Why now is a GREAT time to invest in Crystal Palace" piece. *This is
  our planned buy.*
- **Woltemade — still selling well, ~307,000 in.** The GW8 Newcastle bandwagon kept
  a third week of momentum, though now behind Mbeumo/Mateta.
- **Sarr — the Palace *differential* route** for those wanting Palace exposure but
  fearing Mateta's rotation/ownership; flagged as Palace's rotation-immune goal
  threat into the soft run (WOL, BUR home games ahead). Named alongside Mateta/Mbeumo
  as the week's lead buys.

**Out:**
- **João Pedro — most-transferred-out, ~862,000 out.** Chelsea's early-season star
  (2 goals, 3 assists in his first four) had gone four straight without a return;
  his midweek European ban guaranteed a Saturday start v Sunderland, but the crowd
  had seen enough and rotated the budget into Mateta. FFScout's own read was
  actually "probably worth retaining" given SUN/tot/WOL/bur ahead — a rare case of
  the site fading the herd.
- **Reijnders — ~309,000 out**, the most-sold midfielder, funding the Mbeumo/Sarr
  midfield moves.
- **Salah — ~263,000 out** and still bleeding owners. Now the alarming development:
  he had **not started his last run of matches** (bench/cameo minutes), turning the
  season-long "sell Salah" trickle into a minutes-driven exodus; price sliding.
  **Kudus** also among the most-sold mids.

## 3. Strategy roadmaps in circulation that week

- **"Load up on Palace" was the dominant roadmap.** The headline strategic move of
  the week was pre-empting **Crystal Palace's six-game run** (premierleague "Why now
  is a GREAT time to invest in Crystal Palace"; FFF GW10 transfers). Managers were
  **doubling and even tripling up** on Palace assets — Mateta the template forward,
  **Sarr the differential midfielder**, with Palace facing bottom-half/leaky
  defences (Wolves, Burnley) and home games v Brentford, Brighton and Man United to
  come. Palace's Europa Conference commitments were the one caveat, judged
  acceptable from GW10 on.
- **Fixture-run targets:** **Arsenal** still marquee (BUR A the softest game on the
  board — Saka/Gabriel/Raya the routes; six clean sheets in nine). **Man United**
  fixtures turned (MUN at Forest A) — Mbeumo the owned peak-bandwagon route.
  **Brighton's** run "looks great up until GW16" — **Welbeck** (5 goals in 4, £6.4m,
  ~5.4% owned, on pens) the standout budget differential, with **Igor Thiago**
  (£6.2m; 22 shots, 3rd for big chances behind Haaland/Mateta) the value forward.
- **Differentials gaining traction (sub-10% owned):** **Welbeck** (Brighton),
  **Sarr** (Palace), **Thiago** (Brentford), **Guimarães** (Newcastle's joint-top
  Fantasy mid, level with Salah/Gakpo on 42 pts — the Scout radar's differential
  midfield tip). rotowire led on **Matheus Cunha** as a beat-the-template shout.
- **Chip talk — quiet.** No significant early Bench Boost / Wildcard chatter this
  week: most managers had banked their first Wildcard around the GW8 international
  break, and GW10 was a settled single-transfer round. Chip content pointed at the
  *winter* (Free Hit / Bench Boost held for the later Blank/Double Gameweeks, which
  cup ties would create). No DGW/BGW in the immediate window.
- **Template reveal — FPL General's GW10 (FFScout, 2025-11-01):** still fully
  committed to his **no-Salah, no-Haaland** structure; his stated priority was
  **fixing the forward line** and he was **bringing in Gabriel** (not having the
  Arsenal defender "did more damage"). Consistent with the Arsenal-defence and
  Palace-forward themes of the week. (Dated deadline day; squad/plans pre-deadline,
  any result figures alongside would be post-deadline.)

## 4. Effective-ownership context for OUR holds

- **Haaland (we hold, captain — HOME v Bournemouth):** the easy week. After the
  Villa blank the crowd's answer to "is he still the automatic captain?" was **yes,
  emphatically** — home to Bournemouth is the textbook bounce-back spot, he leads
  the league in shots-in-box/big-chances/xG over the last six, and the FFScout poll
  kept him #1. Captaining Haaland is the zero-EO-risk move; **Saka at Burnley (A)**
  is the only defensible alternative and it is a fade, not consensus. Captain Haaland;
  log Saka as the live-but-not-required hedge.
- **B. Fernandes + Mbeumo (we hold — MUN at Forest, A):** **Mbeumo was the single
  most-bought player in the game this week (~961k in)** — holding him is maximally
  EO-positive and we are on the right side of the biggest bandwagon of GW10. Forest
  away is a shade tougher than GW9's Brighton-at-home, but United's fixtures were the
  consensus "turning" story and both our United routes are the *owned* ones. No
  action; hold and benefit.
- **Semenyo + Senesi (we hold — BOU AT Man City, A):** the muted week. Semenyo drops
  from GW9's #2-captain all the way out of the frame because Bournemouth are **away
  at Man City**, facing Haaland's clean sheet. He is still near-template so a blank
  is a low-EO "everyone owns him" event rather than a differential hit — but there is
  **no tailwind and real downside risk to a Senesi clean sheet** (Bournemouth
  keeping one at the Etihad is unlikely). No consensus pressure to sell either, but
  this is a hold-and-hope-not week, not a hold-and-benefit one. If anything, our
  Palace/United exposure is where the week's points are meant to come from.
- **Ekitiké (we hold — LIV v Villa, H — we PLAN to SELL him for Mateta):** the
  judgment call. **Buying Mateta is the single most consensus-aligned move in the
  game this week** (the ~37.7% João Pedro → Mateta migration; 2nd most-bought
  player; the Palace-run roadmap). So the *destination* of our swap is textbook
  consensus. The *source* is where we diverge from the herd: the crowd was funding
  Mateta out of **João Pedro / Salah / Reijnders**, **not** Ekitiké — who was **not**
  among the most-sold, remained a widely-owned (~30%+) premium forward, was **rested
  midweek**, and had a **home fixture v Aston Villa**. FFScout/allaboutfpl even
  flagged Ekitiké as a "worth-a-gamble" rested home striker despite Liverpool's poor
  form. So selling Ekitiké specifically carries **mild EO risk**: if Liverpool's
  attack fires at home and Ekitiké is the one who hauls, we are offside on a popular
  hold while the field kept him. The counter — and why the swap is still defensible —
  is that we are moving budget *out of Liverpool's wobble into the game's hottest
  fixture run*, capturing Mateta before a documented **price rise** (he ticked up
  ~£0.2m around this window; "buy before he rises" was an explicit pressure). Net
  read: **destination = full consensus; source = a shade contrarian**. The move is
  Moneyball-sound (Mateta's £/underlying > Ekitiké's on this fixture swing) but log
  the Ekitiké-home-v-Villa EO exposure as the known risk we are accepting.
