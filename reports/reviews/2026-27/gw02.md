## 2. The decisions — graded

### 2.1 The roll (0 transfers) — **PASS, and the counterfactual confirms it**

The declined move was **Thiaw → De Cuyper**, refused on the standing rule *never enter a
target into its worst fixture* (Brighton's GW2 was Chelsea away, FDR4, De Cuyper's joint-worst
of the next four). Grading the counterfactual on verified GW2 events:

- **Thiaw: clean sheet, Newcastle won 2-0 away at Tottenham.** Floor 6, central 7.
- **De Cuyper: Brighton lost 4-3 at home to Chelsea, no clean sheet.** The verified
  goalscorers for Brighton, cross-checked against two independent, source-attributed match
  reports (Sky Sports, Chelsea FC's own report, both naming Lavia/Neto/João Pedro/Palmer for
  Chelsea and **Yalcouyé, an own goal deflected in off João Pedro, and Groß** for Brighton),
  do **not** include De Cuyper. A single AI-search-synthesis snippet claimed "De Cuyper
  scored in the 18th minute" — checked against the two corroborating, named-scorer sources
  and rejected as the kind of contamination described in §4. He may have had an unconfirmed
  assist on the deflected cross; even generously credited, that is an appearance (2) + assist
  (3) = 5, still below Thiaw's 6.

**The declined swap was the right call on the fixture it was declined for, twice over now:**
right on the rule (GW1's Bournemouth precedent), right on GW2's actual result. Rolling to 2
FT for GW3, banked specifically for the paired move the plan named, is vindicated — see
`decisions/gw03.md`.

### 2.2 Captain Haaland — **PASS on process, PASS on outcome, adherence 2/2**

Highest projection by 1.3 over the next name (Mbeumo, 4.7), backed by the corrected §2.1

override was needed because none was tempting — this is the rule working exactly as

### 2.5 Chip: NONE — **right process, a real cost this time, logged as variance not error**

Per Moneyball rule 3, this is graded on process, not the outcome it left on the table.

- **Triple Captain was explicitly considered and declined** in `gw02.md` §5: *"City's
  COV(H) (FDR 2) in GW3 is strictly better [than CRY(A) FDR3]... Standing rule: TC on a
  standout fixture or a DGW. Not met."* Haaland's actual GW2 return (a brace, ~20-24 pts
  doubled) would have made a fired TC worth roughly another 10-12 points on top. **That is
  a real, nameable cost of discipline** — but the decision was made on Wednesday's
  information (an FDR-3 away fixture, no DGW), not on Friday's brace, and the same
  discipline is what stopped a TC from being burned on GW1's blank. Judged at decision
  time, holding was correct; judged on outcome, it was expensive. Both are true and neither
  overrides the other — that is what "process over outcome" means in practice, including
  when the outcome you missed was good, not just when it was bad.
- **Bench Boost, Wildcard, Free Hit** — none met their trigger (weak bench, no injury
  crisis, no blank/double). No new information changes that.

## 4. A genuine new finding: search-engine contamination in a fictional future season

Both research agents this session independently hit and flagged the same failure mode:
web search for "2026/27 season" news returns results contaminated with **real, past-season
news bleeding through** — swapped club names (Everton's injury list attributed to "West
Ham"), stale dates (a 2023 Schade injury, Feb–Apr 2026 Man Utd items appearing in a
September query), and directly conflicting scorelines for the same fixture reported by
different search snippets (Chelsea–Brighton returned as 3-0, 4-3, 3-1 and 4-1 depending on
the query). One single-source claim ("De Cuyper scored") was caught and rejected only
because it was cross-checked against two independently-sourced, named-scorer match reports
(§2.1) — without that check it would have gone into this review as a false "the fixture
rule cost us a goal" finding.

**This is a standing process risk, not a one-off.** The current practice (verify against
multiple sources, prefer named/dated/sourced match reports over AI-search-synthesis
sentences) caught it this time. Made explicit as a rule below.

## 6. Three lessons that change next week's behavior

1. **The projections pipeline has now not run live for two consecutive gameweeks.**
   Both memos have been hand-derived carry-forwards, both explicitly labelled as such, and
   both good enough for the *low-stakes* decisions they supported (a roll, a hold) but
   neither would be good enough for a multi-move reshape. GW3's queued paired move
   (`decisions/gw03.md`) is exactly the kind of decision this blocker starts to bite on —
   arming the local-machine automation (STATE.md backlog item, flagged since GW1) is now a
   two-gameweek-overdue priority, not a nice-to-have.
2. **Add DefCon (defensive contribution) to the model and the review.** It is a live,
   real scoring category this season, we own four players who plausibly earn it every week
   (Tarkowski, Gabriel, Thiaw, Semenyo), and it is currently invisible to both the
   projections pipeline and this review. Until it is sourced, defender projections are
   undercounting a real, structural points category — not noise, a known gap.
3. **Cross-check every single-source web claim against a second, independently-attributed
   source before it enters a memo — make this explicit, not just habitual.** Two separate
   research passes this session hit real season-contamination (§4); the one that would have
   produced a wrong finding (De Cuyper's phantom goal) was caught only because the habit was
   already there. Write it into the news-sweep skill as a standing step rather than relying
   on each session to reinvent it.
