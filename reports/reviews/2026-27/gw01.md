## 2. The decisions — graded

### 2.1 The World Cup fade thesis — **FAIL on the mechanism**, and the mechanism is the lesson

The memo's whole build rested on one forecast: ~40 FPL-relevant players reach the deadline
with ~10 days of club training and no FPL flag, so **fade them**. Fifty-six hand-written
overlays encoded that as a **minutes** risk — reduced start probability, duration 3.

**Every named returner started.** Guéhi 90', O'Reilly 79', E. Anderson 84' for City; Saka
(scored), Rice, White (assist), Havertz (scored), Ødegaard (scored) for Arsenal. Not one
was rested. Three of the players we explicitly named as fades returned in GW1:
**Guéhi (goal), Saka (goal), Rogers (goal on his Chelsea debut).**

But the underlying read was *not* wrong — it was filed under the wrong variable. Maresca
said on the record that Haaland and the other returners "only returned around 10 days ago",
describing this stage as **an extension of pre-season, with a little rust**. Haaland then
took **0.85 xG — over a third of City's 2.36 — and scored none.**

> **The update: post-tournament fatigue in 2026/27 is a finishing/sharpness effect, not a
> minutes effect.** Managers are starting the returners and accepting the rust. Encoding it
> as minutes made us fade players who played 90 and returned. Encoding it as sharpness would
> have said *own the returners, but do not captain the rustiest of them* — which is close to
> the inverse of what we actually did.

**Action taken:** the duration-3 WC minutes overlays still cover GW2–3. They are **retired**
as of the GW2 memo. Nothing replaces them; one round of evidence says the market read the
minutes correctly and we did not.

### 2.2 The B.Fernandes fade — **PASS**, right for the right reason, and it cost nothing

B.Fernandes started, blanked, Man Utd lost 2-0 at Hull: ~2 pts against FFS's 6.5 projection.
Mbeumo, the £8.0m asset we bought instead on the identical fixture, also returned ~2.
**Net on the week: zero — but we hold the same output for £4.0m less.** The written hedge
("we own his team-mate on the identical HUL(A)+IPS(H) opening, so the weeks Bruno hauls are
largely the weeks our Man Utd asset returns too") worked exactly as specified: this week
neither hauled. 51.9% of the field carried the more expensive version of the same blank.
**The fade holds into GW2.**

### 2.3 The named positional duel — **FAIL**

Named bet: *"Mbeumo away at Hull (FDR 2) — a fit, sharp, pre-season-complete winger against
a newly promoted defence."* Hull won 2-0; Mbeumo's best chance was saved by debutant keeper
Tzolakis; Man Utd were, in the reports' word, lacklustre.

The lesson is **not** "stop naming duels". It is that this duel priced the **player** and
ignored the **team**. Mbeumo's individual matchup was as described; Man Utd's system was the
variable that decided the game, and the duel lens has no term for it. Duel record: 2 buy-side
hits (backtest) + 1 miss. **New constraint: a named duel must clear a team-level check —
do not name a duel for an attacker whose side is the fixture's underdog on the underlying.**

### 2.4 Captain Haaland — **PASS on process**, −4.3 on outcome, adherence 1/1

Our highest projection by a 4.1-point margin, 69.3% owned, the most-captained player in the
game, and he generated 0.85 xG. He blanked *with the field*, which is a shared cost, not a
rank cost. Moneyball rule 3: this is variance, logged as good process. No recency override.

### 2.5 The iteration-2 self-correction — **PASS, and measurably so**

Iteration 1 started **Matheus Nunes** (MCI, £6.0m) while the same memo argued against
O'Reilly on the grounds that Maresca's box midfield suppresses City full-backs' attacking
returns. The rule was written and not applied to the player the solver picked; iteration 2
swapped in **Tarkowski** at the same price.

**Nunes came off the bench at ~81' — roughly 1 point. Tarkowski started 90 and kept a clean
sheet — 6+.** The correction was worth about **+5 on the week**, having been costed at
"−0.20 objective, +0.49 GW1 xPts — effectively free". Arguing against your own solver paid.

### 2.6 The Raya call — **PASS**, the best single line in the overlay

*"A World Cup champion with Spain who played zero minutes in the entire tournament: a
champion by medal, a spectator by workload."* He started and kept a clean sheet behind the
softest fixture on the slate. He was 37.6% owned at the deadline, so not the differential the
July snapshot implied — but the reasoning was independent of the crowd and correct.

### 2.7 The Bournemouth ban — **PASS on the rule, mixed on the sample**

We banned every Bournemouth asset on *buy runs of fixtures, and never enter into the target's
worst fixture* (BOU opened at Man City away). **Truffert** — the asset the optimizer most
wanted — started, lost 2-1, no clean sheet, ~1–2 pts. The rule was right on him.
**Tavernier scored**, so the ban cost us there. Net roughly neutral. The rule stands; it is
re-applied in the GW2 memo to a different target (§2.8, and `decisions/gw02.md`).

### 2.8 What we missed — both misses were the *same* miss

| Player | £m | GW1 | Why we didn't have him |
|---|---|---|---|
| **De Cuyper** (BHA) | 4.5 | **17** — goal, assist, CS, 2 bonus; 13.9 xFPL, joint-top for big chances | Not on any shortlist |
| **Guéhi** (MCI) | — | ~11 — goal, 90' | Named fade: "England semi-finalist" |

De Cuyper is an FPL-classified **defender deployed on the left wing**. Guéhi is an
FPL-classified **defender who started in central midfield** under Maresca and scored.
Both are one edge: **a player whose FPL position is cheaper than the role he actually
plays.** We wrote 56 overlays about tournament fatigue and zero about positional
classification, and the two biggest hauls of the gameweek were both positional arbitrage.

Worse, the evidence was available and we chose not to look: the memo's own plan said *"the
Arsenal question resolves itself on 16 August — the Community Shield is the only competitive
fixture in which the late returners can actually be seen starting."* The Community Shield
(ARS v MCI, 16 Aug) would have shown Guéhi in midfield. **Nobody watched it.** See §2.9.

### 2.9 **PROCESS FAILURE — the T-48h re-solve never ran.** This is the biggest finding.

The GW1 memo committed, in writing, three times:
- *"This is an early iteration of the season's opening squad, not a final submission... it will be re-solved at T-48h against actual team news."*
- *"Owner action required: none yet. Do not enter this in the FPL app today."*
- *"Revisit [B.Fernandes] at T-48h and at GW2–3 — this is the line likeliest to change."*

Between the memo (2026-07-26) and the deadline (2026-08-21) **no session ran.** A 26-day-old
draft, labelled by its own author as not a submission, was the team that played. We got away
with it — the base XI beat its projection — but the miss is structural, not lucky: the
Community Shield went unwatched, the press conferences went unread, and the falsification of
the entire thesis (§2.1) was observable five days before the deadline.

Moneyball rule 5 says *never miss a deadline*. We did not miss the deadline; we missed every
checkpoint we set ourselves before it. **Fix: arm the scheduled sessions (Phase 5 automation).
It has been in the backlog since July and is now the highest-value item in the repo.**
