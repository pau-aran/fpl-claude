## 1. The transfer

### 1.1 Thiaw → De Cuyper — funded, well-evidenced, executed

**The case, largely unchanged from the GW2 memo's declined-then-queued version, now with two
new confirmations from this week's sweep:**

- **Fixture case intact and now live.** Brighton play **Leeds (H), FDR2**, then **Coventry
  (A), FDR2** at GW4 — the run this move exists for starts this week, not next.
- **Role risk resolved, mildly favorably.** Brighton signed left-back **Jaouen Hadjam**
  (~£10-12m, ex-Young Boys) in late August, which could have squeezed De Cuyper's advanced
  left-wing role. It didn't: a separate, reportedly-agreed **Kadıoğlu → Man Utd** move did
  **not** complete (Brighton kept him), and the read from two independent sources is that
  De Cuyper keeps the forward left-flank role while Hadjam and the incumbent left-back rotate
  behind him. Net: a name that looked like a threat to the role turned out not to be one.
- **GW2 result graded the decline, not the buy, and it graded PASS** — De Cuyper did not
  score in Brighton's 4-3 home loss to Chelsea (verified against two independently-sourced,
  named-scorer match reports; see the GW2 review §2.1 and §4 for why a contradicting
  single-source "he scored" claim was rejected). This is not a reason to buy him now — it is
  the confirmation that declining him *for that fixture* was correct, which is a different
  question from whether he is worth owning for *this* fixture. He still is, on the fixture
  run, independent of last week's box score.

**The funding problem, stated honestly.** De Cuyper's confirmed price at the GW2 deadline
was £4.6m; this sweep could not retrieve a reliable post-GW2 price (his GW2 involvement was
unremarkable per the review, so a further rise is possible but not the near-certainty a
second haul would have made it). Thiaw sells at £5.0m (no evidence of a price move on a
2.6%-owned asset). **The trigger the GW2 memo pre-committed to — "if he rises past £4.9m,
revisit" — is therefore the live gate on this move, and it cannot be checked from this
sandbox.**

**Plan B, explicit:** if De Cuyper is priced at or below £5.0m in the app at entry, execute
as planned (funds itself, ~£0.0–0.4m surplus). **If he is priced above £5.0m, hold Thiaw
instead and roll both FT to GW4** — the fixture case survives one more week (Brighton's run
doesn't end at GW3), and a shortfall this small is not worth financing by selling a second
player. Do not improvise a different destination for the FT at T-2h; either take this move
as costed or don't take it.

### 1.2 Mbeumo → [Liverpool third slot] — **DEFERRED, not declined**

The GW2 memo's queued second leg was **Mbeumo → Kerkez or Frimpong**, on the read that Man
Utd's fixtures turn hard after GW2 (EVE A, MCI H, FUL A) and that Liverpool's is the best run
in the league. That fixture read is now confirmed exactly as written — Man Utd's GW3 is
Everton away, FDR3, the first of the three. **The move is not executed this week, for three
reasons that are about the strength of the evidence, not the strength of the fixture case:**

1. **The fixture case was always the whole argument, and this week complicated it.** Mbeumo
   scored (82', assisted by Bruno Fernandes) in a **5-2 home win over Ipswich in which Bruno
   Fernandes scored a hat-trick.** A Man Utd front three producing five goals together, with
   two different players combining for a goal, is a genuine data point that the attack may be
   less fixture-dependent than the pure FDR read assumed — or it may just be one good week
   against a promoted side, which is exactly the kind of question the plan's own precondition
   ("solve it properly... with real projections") exists to settle and which cannot be settled
   by eye from one match.
2. **The destination has a fresh, real flag of its own.** Kerkez and Frimpong were both
   named in one source as "particularly shocking" defensively in a recent match, with both
   picking up yellow cards. Not disqualifying — but it is new information against the buy,
   arriving the same week as new information against the sell, and two fresh uncertainties
   compounding is a worse basis for a move than one.
3. **Neither price is verified.** Unlike the De Cuyper leg (a single, small, boundable
   funding risk with a pre-written trigger), this leg's funding math has never been costed
   against real prices — the GW2 memo said only that it would be "solved properly" at GW3.
   Executing an unpriced, unsolved two-way swap on hand-derived judgment, in the same week the
   evidence for it got mixed rather than confirmed, is exactly the kind of decision Moneyball
   rule 3 asks to be defensible with data *and* a written rationale before the fact — this one
   currently has neither at the confidence level the other leg has.

**This is deferred, not refused.** The FT banks to GW4 rather than being spent or lost
(rolling to the cap is legal and free). Re-open at GW4 with two more pieces of evidence in
hand that don't exist yet: whether Mbeumo produces against Everton away (a genuine
fixture-difficulty test, not a home game vs a promoted side) and whether the pipeline can be
run for a real solve by then.

  single-fixture case City will offer without a double gameweek. Declined anyway: (a) it is

  committed grid: SUN(H) at GW5, IPS(H) at GW7) — using the first TC of the season in GW3 on

  waiting; (c) GW2's review explicitly logged the discipline cost of holding through a brace
  (§5 there) — the point of writing that down was to keep applying the same rule, not to
  relitigate it the first time a similarly good fixture appears. **Named and declined, not
  silently skipped**, so next week's review can grade this the same way it graded GW2's.
- **Bench Boost** — bench is van Ewijk, O'Shea, Obi (still not in Man Utd's matchday squad
  through two gameweeks), Heaton (third-choice keeper, unused twice). Worth perhaps 4-6
  points. No.
- **Wildcard / Free Hit** — no blank or double gameweek, no injury crisis, and the squad has
  now outscored its own hand-derived projection in back-to-back weeks. Nothing to fix.

## 6. Risks, stated plainly

1. **De Cuyper's live price is unverified and is the binding constraint on this week's only
   move.** Plan B (§1.1) is explicit: hold Thiaw instead if he's priced above £5.0m at entry.
2. **The Mbeumo deferral is a bet that this week's evidence (his goal, Bruno's hat-trick) is
   noise and the fixture-difficulty thesis still holds — or it is a bet that we don't yet
   know which, and choosing not to act on an unpriced, unsolved swap is the more disciplined
   read either way.** If Man Utd's front three keeps producing against EVE(A)/MCI(H)/FUL(A),
   this deferral costs real points and should be logged as a genuine miss, not excused as
   process. If it doesn't, the deferral was free and the GW4 solve (hopefully with real
   projections) executes the move a week later at no cost beyond one week's delay.
3. **Arsenal's hard fixture (CHE H, FDR4) is held through, not reacted to** — consistent
   with `gw02.md`'s read that the Arsenal question is a GW4-6 question (their run softens at
   GW6, LEE H, FDR2), not a one-bad-week reaction. Raya and Gabriel both start.
4. **DefCon points remain completely unmodelled** (GW2 review §1) for four owned defenders.
   Not actionable this week, carried forward as a standing model gap.
5. **The pipeline has not run live for three consecutive gameweeks.** Every number and
   funding calculation in this memo is hand-derived and explicitly labelled as such. The
   discipline of only taking the *self-funding, well-evidenced* half of a two-leg plan (§1)
   is this memo's answer to that constraint, not a fix for it.
6. **An unverified "Isak to Liverpool" transfer surfaced in this week's sweep**, alongside a
   batch of other transfer headlines flagged as high-risk for being stale/misdated
   (real-world 2025 news bleeding into 2026/27 search results — see the GW2 review §4 for the
   same pattern). Not acted on. If Liverpool's attack has genuinely gained another forward,
   it is relevant to Wirtz's service and worth a clean re-check next week, not this one.
7. **Search-contamination remains a live data-quality risk** (GW2 review §4) — every claim in
   this memo that could plausibly be stale (De Cuyper's exact price, Kerkez/Frimpong's
   defensive-solidity flag) was flagged with its confidence level rather than stated flatly.
