## 1. What the 5-gameweek horizon actually changed

Run both horizons on the *same* snapshot and the *same* overlay, so the only difference is
the horizon:

| | Players |

| In **both** squads (9 of 15) | Kelleher, Tarkowski, Gvardiol, Calafiori, Thiaw, Semenyo, Mbeumo, Haaland, Crooks |

| Only the **5-GW** squad picks | **B.Fernandes**, Schade, Calvert-Lewin, O'Shea, Heaton, Neave |

**The headline is one player: the 5-gameweek horizon buys Bruno Fernandes.**

Man Utd's opening pair is **Hull away (FDR 2) and Ipswich home (FDR 2)** — the softest two
fixtures any club has, and they expire. Their GW6-8 is Tottenham home, Leeds away,
Bournemouth home, while Arsenal's and Chelsea's runs *improve* over the same stretch. Over
eight gameweeks the £12.0m is not worth it and iteration 2 faded him on exactly that
arithmetic (2.10 pts/£m against Mbeumo's 2.72). Over five it is worth it, because the part
of Man Utd's schedule that drags the average down has been cut out of the question.

A caveat, stated because it constrains how much of this to believe: **the horizon change is
small in the mean and decisive only at the margin.** With the ruleset's 0.85 decay, GW6-8
already carry only ~23% of the weight, and the club-level ratio of 5-GW to 8-GW projection
runs from 0.747 (Coventry) to 0.777 (Leeds) — a ±2% spread around the 0.764 a neutral
fixture profile would give. So the horizon does not re-rank the league. What it does is
move a handful of players across the budget's knife-edge, and the £12.0m slot is the most
knife-edged decision on the board.

**The verdict is spend it, and here is why that is an answer rather than a default:**

1. **The frontier has no flat region.** It costs a near-constant **~1.05 decayed 5-GW points
   per £1.0m banked**, all the way down. There is no knee to stop at — the budget constraint
   binds at every level (spend equals the cap in every row). That is the specific thing the
   owner's instruction asked us to check, and it is not what we hoped to find.
2. **The XI column is the honest one and it agrees.** The objective also prices the bench at
   10%, and at a season open the bench is four players the model genuinely cannot rank
   (defect D7 — every priorless player gets the same positional replacement number). Reported
   separately, the XI-only 5-GW loss tracks the objective almost exactly (−4.29 vs −4.39 at
   £96.0m), so the frontier is not a bench artefact.
3. **This XI cannot be bought for less.** Lock the eleven and re-solve for the cheapest legal
   fifteen and the answer is **£100.0m exactly, infeasible at £99.5m** — because the bench is
   already at FPL's price floor (§Decision). There is no slack to bank; every pound removed
   comes out of the team that plays.
4. Reading it as a price: £4.0m of bank costs ~4.3 decayed points, roughly **6 raw points
   across GW1-5**. Bank scores nothing on its own, and this squad's flexibility problem is
   free transfers, not cash — the GW2-3 reversals in §6 are like-for-like in money.

If the owner wants a token buffer anyway, **£99.5m for −0.48** is the cheapest step on the
board (it downgrades Kelleher £5.0m → Leno £4.5m and touches nothing else). It is not
recommended, but it is the one step that is nearly free.

Three things fall out of that table.

**The B.Fernandes question is now a coin flip, and that is the argument for owning him.**
V1 beats V4 by **0.064 objective points** and 0.18 GW1 XI points; on the XI-only horizon V4
is actually *ahead* by 0.11. These are the same squad to within any precision our model can
defend at a season rollover. Iteration 2 faded him on a genuine ~0.8-1.0 gap over eight
gameweeks and was right to. On five gameweeks that gap has closed to nothing — and when EV
is a tie, the tie-break is not EV, it is rank variance. He is owned by **48.6%** of the
game. Declining to own a coin-flip asset that half the field holds is paying a real risk
premium for zero expected return, and Moneyball rule 1 is about *value*, not about being
contrarian for its own sake. **He comes in.**

**V4 is a real squad, not a straw man**, and the owner should see what the fade would buy.
It is a five-player reshape, not a one-for-one: out go **B.Fernandes, Schade, Calvert-Lewin,
O'Shea and Neave**, in come **Gabriel (£8.0m), Wirtz (£7.5m), Thiago (£8.0m), Obi and
McConnell** — a broader, less concentrated build with three £8.0m-class assets instead of
one £12.0m, and it drops Man Utd from three players to two. If the T-2h news puts Bruno's
minutes in doubt, V4 is the fallback, already solved.

**Haaland is the only lock on the board.** Removing him costs 4.19 — an order of magnitude
more than any other single-player constraint. At 75.0% owned he is not a consensus-follow;
he is our own top projection by a 3.3-point margin over the next name, and the captaincy
anchor. Captain follows the standing rule (highest GW1 projection, no recency override).

## 5. The manager overlay — what we override and why

**Bournemouth: banned, and the 5-gameweek view makes the ban stronger.** The raw solver
wanted Truffert *and* Tavernier in the XI. Bournemouth's GW1-5 is MCI(A)5, EVE(H)3, NEW(A)3,
BRE(H)3, LIV(H)4 — summed FDR **18, the joint-worst in the league** — and GW1 is Man City
away, their single worst fixture. The standing rule is *buy runs of fixtures, and never
enter into the target's worst fixture*. Over eight gameweeks their run improves (GW6-8 adds
only 10); cutting the horizon to five removes exactly the part that made them defensible.
Cost of the discipline, measured on this table: **0.78 objective points** (191.435 unbanned
against 190.658). They are a GW6+ watch, and with prices frozen we lose nothing by waiting.

**What we own that the crowd does not:**

- **Kelleher (£5.0m, 5.6%) ahead of Raya (£6.0m, 30.7%).** This reverses iteration 2's
  signature pick, and not because Raya was wrong — he started the Community Shield and the
  "World Cup champion who played zero minutes" read was correct. It is priced out: Raya
  gives 13.40 over five gameweeks against Kelleher's 12.70, so the extra £1.0m buys **0.70
  points — below the £1.05-per-£1.0m rate the frontier says that million is worth
  elsewhere**. Locking Raya costs 0.37 objective (V2). The right read of iteration 2's Raya
  work is that it won the argument and then the argument stopped mattering.
- **Schade (£6.0m, 2.3%)** — Germany exited at the round of 32, he is in Brentford's
  predicted XI, and he is the best value in the £6.0m midfield bracket.
- **Thiaw (£5.0m, 2.1%)** — in Newcastle's predicted back four, 2.435 pts/£m.
- **Gvardiol (£5.5m, 10.7%)** — see §2. The market is on Guéhi (24.0%) and O'Reilly (22.9%);
  the manager who actually picked a team on 16 August was on Gvardiol.

**What we deliberately do not own:**

- **Guéhi (24.0%), O'Reilly (22.9%), Rice (22.3%), Rogers (30.7%), Szoboszlai (47.6%),
  João Pedro (54.7%), Saka (10.6%).** The first five are the World Cup fade, now
  Shield-tested. The last two are pure value calls: João Pedro costs 0.11 to lock and
  Szoboszlai 0.34, both while being over 45% owned — small enough that if either move is
  wrong it is wrong by a rounding error, and both are named here so the review can grade it.
- **Liverpool, entirely — and this is the line I am least comfortable with.** Liverpool own
  the best GW1-5 run in the league (summed FDR 13) and none of their assets clears the value
  bar: Wirtz 1.865 pts/£m, Virgil 1.853, Szoboszlai 1.829, against Gvardiol's 2.554 and
  Calafiori's 2.548. Their GW1 is *Newcastle away, the hardest of their five*, which is the
  same "do not enter into the worst fixture" logic that has paid before. **The plan is to
  enter Liverpool at GW2, not GW1** (§6).
- **Isak (£9.0m, 11.3%) — owned by nobody here, and the reason is a defect, not a view.**
  He is Liverpool's predicted starting nine and FPL's listed penalty taker, and our table
  projects him **11.69 over five gameweeks — 1.30 pts/£m, the bottom of the premium-forward
  board**, because his prior is 694 minutes and 8 starts from an injury-shortened first Liverpool
  season. That is defect D2 exactly — the minutes prior cannot distinguish "was benched"
  from "was injured". The overlay raises his *minutes* to 0.85 but cannot fix his *per-90
  rates*, which are still blended off that thin sample. **His projection is not
  decision-grade and no decision here rests on it.** He is not excluded on merit; he is
  excluded because we cannot price him, which is a worse reason and is written down as one.

**Positional-duel read** (standing owner directive, named so the review can grade it): the

## 7. Risks, stated plainly

1. **The snapshot's team news is 17 days old, and this is the largest caveat in the memo.**
   The FPL API and every FPL news domain are **egress-blocked in this sandbox** (verified:
   `fantasy.premierleague.com` returns 403 to CONNECT at the proxy). Prices, ownership,
   squads and flags come from a public mirror of the same API whose latest `news_added` is
   **2026-08-03**; prices are frozen and no transfers have been recorded, so identity and
   money are current, but *flags are not*. Everything in §2 is second-hand, from web-search
   summaries of the FPL press, cross-checked against the snapshot's own squad lists before
   being written down. That check earned its keep: it caught a summarised Arsenal XI naming
   a midfielder who is not in the squad, a Leeds keeper who is on another club's roster
   here, and a Man Utd fixture that does not exist in GW1. **Anything the owner can see in
   the live FPL app at T-2h beats anything in this memo.**
2. **Six of fifteen come from two clubs**, Man City and Man Utd, both at the 3-per-club cap.
   Both have excellent GW1 fixtures; a Man Utd blank at Hull would hurt disproportionately.
3. **Four of fifteen are minutes blind spots** (Neave, O'Shea, Crooks, Heaton) — all four on
   the bench, all four at the price floor, and the model genuinely cannot rank them (D7). The
   sweep is explicit that no £4.5m forward this season is a credible starter, so the fourth
   slot is a dead slot whichever way it is filled. Only **Crooks** carries positive research
   support (he is in Hull's predicted XI) — and note the small conflict that follows: he
   plays *against* our three Man Utd assets in GW1.
4. **Thiaw's GW1 is Liverpool at home (FDR 4)**, his worst of the five. He starts anyway
   because the alternative is bench fodder; if the T-2h news is bad, O'Shea (Sunderland at
   home, FDR 2) is the swap.
5. **Isak is unpriced, not rejected** (§5). If the owner's own read is that a fit, nailed,
   penalty-taking £9.0m striker on the league's best five-gameweek run is worth owning, that
   read is better than our number and should override it.
6. **Bench Boost and Triple Captain are live in GW1 and we are declining both.** If Haaland
   hauls at home to Bournemouth, the TC will look obvious afterwards. The rule is written
   down in advance precisely so that hindsight does not get a vote.
7. **Model caveats carried forward, deliberately unfixed:** D1 (injury flags priced as
   horizon-long absences — mitigated per player in the overlay, and Doku/Mount/Šeško each
   carry an explicit `duration_gws`), D2/D3 (thin and zero-minute priors — the Isak case),
   D7 (no discriminating power over promoted clubs and new signings). All in

## Manager overlay (the human call, outside the algorithm)

**The thesis in one line: the 2026 World Cup ended 19 July and the market has not priced
it, so we fade the tournament's deep runners and buy the clubs that were home early.**

Mandated post-tournament rest runs to ~10–12 August. Roughly forty FPL-relevant players
reach this deadline with about ten days of club training and **no FPL flag of any kind** —
the risk is completely invisible in the FPL UI, which is where our edge has to come from
(Moneyball rule 2: minutes are the market inefficiency). It falls hardest on **Arsenal**
(Raya, Rice, Saka, Eze, Madueke, Zubimendi, Merino, with Saliba injured in the semi-final
and out 4–5 months) and **Man City** (Guéhi, O'Reilly, E. Anderson, Cherki, Trafford, with
Rodri post-surgery) — the two most-templated clubs in the game. **Liverpool are the least
damaged big club** and simultaneously own the best GW1–8 fixture run in the league.

That is the whole build. Fifty-six hand-written minutes overlays implement it
(`decisions/overlays/gw01.json`), each with a written reason and a duration. The durations
matter as much as the shares: the World Cup fatigue calls are **duration-3**, not season
judgements — these are good players who should be fit by GW4, and pricing a transient as
permanent is the overlay-horizon defect that drove three phantom sells in four backtest
weeks last season.

**What we deliberately do NOT own, and why:**

- **B.Fernandes (£12.0m, 47.9% owned) — the biggest call in the memo, and it is a fade.**
  Our own model rates him 2nd in the entire game (25.23 over the horizon) and he is fit,
  penalty-taker, set-piece hub, and has the best opening pair of fixtures in the league.
  This is *not* a doubt about the player. It is opportunity cost: at £12.0m he returns
  **2.10 pts/£m against Mbeumo's 2.72**, and we tested the substitution rather than
  asserting it. Locking him in costs ~0.8–1.0 objective points and, in the variant where he
  is locked alongside Raya and Gabriel, **forces Haaland out of the squad entirely**. The
  EO risk is real and is being taken knowingly — but it is blunted, because **we own
  Mbeumo, his team-mate on the identical HUL(A) + IPS(H) opening**, so the weeks Bruno
  hauls are largely the weeks our Man Utd asset returns too. Moneyball rule 1 applies
  literally here. **Revisit at T-48h and at GW2–3** — this is the line likeliest to change.
- **Rogers (£7.5m, 34.8% owned)** — faded on three independent counts: England
  semi-finalist, a £117m signing who joined Chelsea four days before our snapshot (so no
  pre-season and no system familiarity under a new manager), and he takes **no Chelsea set
  pieces** having been Villa's dead-ball man. Third-most-owned midfielder in the game.
- **Guéhi (25.3%), O'Reilly (24.8%), Porro (23.9%), Rice (22.5%), Konsa (18.4%)** — the
  template's cheap and mid-price core is disproportionately made of England and Spain
  semi-finalists and finalists. We own none of them for GW1.
- **Dubravka (£4.0m, 24.5% owned)** — our raw model ranked him **#1 in the entire game by
  points per million**, purely because it carried 35 starts he made for *Newcastle* into a
  Tottenham shirt he joined on 1 July, aged 37, behind two other keepers. That artifact is
  now fixed in the model and overridden in the overlay. 39.3% of the game's goalkeeper
  exposure is riding on an unresolved three-way battle; we took the knowable option
  instead.
- **All Bournemouth assets** — Truffert is genuinely one of the best value defenders in
  the game and our optimizer wanted him and Tavernier in the XI. Banned anyway:
  Bournemouth have the **worst opening run in the league** (summed FDR 28 over eight, GW1
  is Man City away) and the standing rule is *buy runs of fixtures, and never enter into
  the target's worst fixture*. He is a GW6+ watch, and with prices frozen we lose nothing
  by waiting.
- **All promoted-club goalkeepers** — all three situations are unresolved (Coventry's
  target is unsigned, Hull are chasing two more, Ipswich's £4.0m Palmer lost the shirt in
  October 2025).
- **Lacroix (12.9% owned)** — banned: Chelsea have agreed ~£52m, terms settled, medical
  booked. 12.9% of the game is holding an asset about to be repriced at a different club.

**What we own that the crowd does not, and why:**

- **Raya (£6.0m)** — the single most valuable correction in the overlay. He is a World Cup
  *champion* with Spain who played **zero minutes** in the entire tournament (Unai Simón
  started every game): a champion by medal, a spectator by workload. The market's
  "finalist ⇒ fatigued" heuristic is a false positive on exactly one player, and he is
  Arsenal's nailed No.1 behind the **softest opening fixture on the slate** (Coventry at
  home, FDR 2 v 5 — the biggest gap in GW1). We raised him against a club-wide Arsenal fade.
- **The Liverpool double, Wirtz (12.2%) + Szoboszlai (40.9%)** — best fixture run in the
  league, least World-Cup damage, both confirmed back in training after round-of-32 exits.
- **Schade (£6.0m, 2.3%)** — Germany out at the round of 32, fully rested, and our model's
  best value in the £6.0m midfield bracket.
- **O'Shea (£4.0m, 1.2%)** over **Diop (£4.0m, 21.4%)** — the same price for the Ipswich
  defender who played *every minute* of the promotion season as captain and centre of the
  back three, against one with 812 minutes to his name. Our model is 96% blind on Ipswich,
  so this is entirely research-derived.

**Positional-duel read** (standing owner directive; named so the review can grade it).
The named bet is **Mbeumo away at Hull (FDR 2)** — a fit, sharp, pre-season-complete

run in the division. *Per the standing caution, neither named duel is a pure clean-sheet
bet — those went 0-for-2 last season and now require odds-grade support before naming.*

**Where we agree with the crowd, and why that is fine.** Haaland at 73.7% is not a
consensus-follow, it is our own model's top projection by a 4.1-point margin over the next
name, and he is the captaincy anchor. Not owning him would be a 74%-of-the-field bet
against our own numbers. Captaincy follows the standing rule — highest projection unless
news says otherwise — with no recency override.

## Risks, stated plainly

1. **The World Cup thesis is a forecast, not a fact.** If the deep returners are simply
   started anyway on 21 August, we have faded five of the ten most-owned players in the
   game for nothing. The Community Shield and the 17–20 August press conferences are the
   check, and there is time to reverse every line.
2. **No B.Fernandes.** The single largest ownership exposure we carry. Mitigated by
   Mbeumo, not eliminated.
3. **Four of fifteen are minutes blind spots** (Obi, O'Shea, van Ewijk, Heaton) — all four
   are bench pieces, and all four are cheap enablers at clubs our model is 93–96% blind on.
   None of them starts. This is the honest cost of a season-opening build.
4. **The defence is thin and cheap** behind Gabriel: Tarkowski and Thiaw at £6.0m/£5.0m,
   with two promoted-club defenders as cover. Thiaw's GW1 is Liverpool at home (FDR 4).
5. **A 3-5-2 with two forwards** means an early Haaland or Calvert-Lewin absence pulls in
   Obi, a £4.5m unknown.
6. **Model caveat carried forward, deliberately unfixed**: injury flags are still priced as
   8-GW absences rather than scoped durations. This is mitigated per-player in the overlay —
   every flagged player in this squad's consideration set carries an explicit
   `duration_gws` — and guessing a default duration from a flag would invent information we
   do not have. Documented as D1 in `research/2026-27/model-fixes.md`.

   *The Ipswich club-name mismatch previously listed here is **fixed** (D6). Worth recording
   why it took two attempts: correcting the name alone would have been worse than the bug,
   because `covers()` counted matches without checking their age — Ipswich would then have
   been Dixon-Coles rated on the very relegation season that sent them down. The first fix
   attempt also broke Leeds by expanding a club name FPL leaves bare. Both are now pinned by
   tests in `tests/test_prior_grading.py`.*
