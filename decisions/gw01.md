## The two changes, and why they were forced

### 1. Heaton → Dubravka (GK, £4.0m → £4.0m) — forced

**Tom Heaton is injured.** He picked up a hamstring problem against PSG in pre-season and is
out for at least three more weeks, targeting an early-September return; he is one of eight Man
Utd absentees, alongside Darlow, so an 18-year-old (Fred Heath) is deputising on the bench.
He will carry an FPL flag by the deadline. A flagged £4.0m bench keeper is a dead slot with a
red icon on it — there is no argument for keeping him.

**Replacement: Dubravka (Spurs, £4.0m).** This needs saying plainly, because iteration 2
singled him out as *"the single worst artifact in our projections table, and the reason this
overlay file exists"* — our raw model ranked him **#1 in the entire game** by points per
million on 35 starts he made for *Newcastle* before joining Tottenham on 1 July, aged 37,
behind two other keepers.

That ban is not being reversed; it is being applied to a different question. What was banned
was **starting him, or treating his projection as real**. We are buying him as the fourth bench
slot — a keeper we intend never to play — where the only criteria are *cheap, fit, and most
likely to be useful if our No.1 goes down*. On the last of those he is the best of the £4.0m
field: our own overlay put him at **0.45, the highest start share we assessed for any £4.0m
keeper**, because Vicario missed the pre-season tour with an unflagged minor injury and Spurs'
three-way battle is genuinely open. The alternatives (Steele, Forster, Matthews, Lecomte,
Woodman, Patterson) are settled No.2s with near-zero chance of ever playing, and the promoted
clubs have all since signed keepers who were not in our snapshot — Ipswich's Scherpen, Hull's
Tzolakis, Coventry's Rushworth — which retires Palmer, Phillips and Dovin as £4.0m options.

**No projection is claimed for him.** His model row is the known-corrupt one, and quoting a
number from it would be reinstating the artifact by the back door.

### 2. Obi → Walle Egeli (FWD, £4.5m → £4.5m)

**Man Utd have made Chido Obi available for loan.** He is behind Šeško, Cunha and Mbeumo in
the pecking order, and if he leaves before the deadline the slot is worse than dead. Iteration
2's stated reason for owning him — that cheap, sellable Man Utd bodies keep the 3-per-club cap
from blocking a later B.Fernandes entry — is moot: selling Obi and Heaton *frees* Man Utd cap
space rather than consuming it, leaving us at one (Mbeumo) of three.

**Replacement: Walle Egeli (Ipswich, £4.5m, 2.4% owned).** He is in Ipswich's predicted GW1 XI
as one of the two attacking midfielders, at home to Sunderland (FDR 2), despite being priced as
a forward. The market's £4.5m forward is **Kusi-Asare (Fulham, 8.2%)**, who managed 0 goals and
0 assists in seven substitute appearances last season and whose own model projects ~1.0 point in
GW1, away to Chelsea. Taking a predicted starter on a soft home fixture over a substitute on a
hard one, at the same price, is the bench version of the same rule that governs the XI.

**Also no projection claimed.** Ipswich is a club our model is ~96% blind on; both of these
picks were made on team news, and both sit in bench slots the objective barely weights. The
predicted 51.41 is therefore identical to iteration 2 — *both changes are outside the XI*, and
inventing numbers to make the total move would be false precision.

## What the news validated, changed, and refuted

The whole build rested on one forecast: *the 2026 World Cup ended 19 July, mandated rest ran to
~10–12 August, and the market had not priced the ~40 FPL-relevant players arriving at this
deadline on ~10 days of club training with no FPL flag.* The **Community Shield (Arsenal 3-0
Man City, 16 Aug)** was named in iteration 2 as the one competitive read available before the
deadline. It has now been played. Grading it honestly:

**VALIDATED — the most valuable single call in the overlay.**
**Raya started, kept a clean sheet, and was described as putting in a masterclass.** The thesis
was that the market's "finalist ⇒ fatigued" heuristic was a false positive on exactly one
player — a World Cup champion with Spain who played **zero minutes** because Unai Simón started
every game. He was Arsenal's No.1 on 16 August and he is Arsenal's No.1 at home to Coventry,
the softest fixture on the opening slate. **Gabriel played the full 90** in the same match, and
with **Saliba (back, long-term) and Timber (weeks away) both out**, he is the undisputed leader
of that back line. The two Arsenal picks we raised against a club-wide Arsenal fade are the two
that were most directly confirmed.

**VALIDATED — the rested-and-cheap leg.**
- **Semenyo** started the Community Shield for City and is in every predicted GW1 XI.
- **Haaland** started it too, and is the consensus GW1 captain as well as our own top
  projection by a 1.44-point margin.
- **Schade** is set to start for Brentford, having scored twice and assisted twice in
  pre-season. At £6.0m and 2.3% owned this is the differential the build wanted.
- **Calvert-Lewin** has three pre-season goals (a brace against Liverpool, then the opener
  against Leipzig) and was restored to the XI in Farke's strongest-available final friendly.
- **Wirtz** is Liverpool's No.10 behind Isak in Iraola's 4-2-3-1 — exactly the role the overlay
  assumed, at 12.2% ownership.
- **Thiaw** captained Newcastle in pre-season and is fit.
- **O'Shea** and **van Ewijk** are both in their clubs' predicted XIs. O'Shea's *position* moved
  — he is at full-back with Diop and Greaves as the centre-backs, not "centre of the back
  three" as iteration 2 argued — so the specific reasoning was wrong while the conclusion
  (he starts, Diop's 21.4% ownership is the worse-informed side of the trade) survives.

**REFUTED — and this is the honest failure of the build.**
**The World Cup fade over-corrected on the Arsenal and Man City returners' minutes.** Rice,
Saka and Zubimendi were named on the Community Shield *bench* having not featured in pre-season
at all — the fatigue was real — but all three are now expected to **start** against Coventry.
**O'Reilly started** the Community Shield outright, and **Guéhi** is in City's predicted XI.
Our overlay had Rice at 0.52, Saka at 0.50, Guéhi at 0.50 and O'Reilly at 0.38. Those numbers
were too harsh: the correct read was "returns under-cooked into a congested opening", not
"does not play".

Two things follow, and only one of them is a squad change:
1. **No squad change.** We own none of those four, so the cost is opportunity, not damage, and
   buying any of them now would mean breaking a news-validated pick to fund it (see the
   refused package below). The structural half of the thesis — buy the clubs that came home
   early, and buy the freshest keeper — is what actually put points in the XI.
2. **It is a calibration entry, logged for `/fpl-review`.** The duration-3 scoping was right;
   the *depth* of the cut was not. Rice in particular is a GW2–4 buy candidate — though note
   that Arsenal's midfield has since absorbed **Bruno Guimarães (£75m from Newcastle)** on top
   of Ødegaard, Zubimendi, Merino and Lewis-Skelly, so the rotation half of the Rice fade has
   if anything strengthened even as the fitness half collapsed.

## The two open questions from iteration 2, closed

### B.Fernandes (£12.0m, ~48% owned) — **the fade stands**

STATE.md flagged this as the line likeliest to change at T-48h. It does not change, and the
reason is that **nothing in the news moved his number**. Our overlay already had him at
**0.88 — the highest start share we assigned to any midfielder** — already assuming he is Man
Utd's nailed No.10 and penalty taker on the best opening pair of fixtures in the league
(HUL away FDR 2, then IPS home FDR 2). The team news says exactly that: *"Bruno Fernandes
remains the undisputed starter at number 10."* A confirmation of an assumption we had already
made at full strength is not new information, and re-deciding on it would be recency, not
evidence.

The arithmetic that drove the original fade is therefore untouched: **2.10 pts/£m against
Mbeumo's 2.72**, tested across five constrained solves, one of which showed that locking him
in alongside Raya and Gabriel **forces Haaland out of the squad entirely**.

What *has* changed is in our favour. **Mbeumo is playing as the striker**, because Šeško is out
with a shin injury — so our £8.0m midfield-priced asset is taking the centre-forward's chances
on the same HUL(A) + IPS(H) opening, at two-thirds of Bruno's price. The hedge got better, not
worse.

This is knowingly the season's largest single EO exposure — roughly half the field owns him,
and his fixtures are the best on the board. If he hauls at Hull we lose ground in week one. We
are taking it on Moneyball rule 1, with the hedge named, and it is the first line to revisit at
GW3 when Man Utd's opening pair expires (EVE away, then MCI home).

### Szoboszlai vs Thiago — **tested properly, and refused**

Igor Thiago looked like the strongest available upgrade, and on the surface the case was loud:
22 Premier League goals last season (second only to Haaland), Brentford's penalty #1, four
attacking returns in two pre-season matches, and **home to Spurs** in GW1. Our pre-overlay
table rated him the best value in the pool at **2.80 xPts/£m**. Szoboszlai, meanwhile, is
40.9% owned, has no penalty duty (the FPL API lists Isak as Liverpool's #1), is **away at
Newcastle** — Liverpool's hardest of the opening four — and is being deployed **deep, in a
double pivot alongside Gravenberch**, not in the advanced role his priors were built on.

It does not survive being priced on the same scale as the rest of the squad. That 2.80 is a
**pre-overlay** figure; the committed squad's numbers are all **post-overlay**, and comparing
across the two is the error the whole exercise had to avoid. `notebooks/gw01_final_overlay_scale.py`
recovers the transform the 2026-07-26 solve applied (it reproduces the committed post-overlay
values to within 1.7%, four of five inside 0.8%) and on that scale:

And the deep-pivot worry turns out to cut the other way: a double-pivot midfielder is where
**defensive-contribution points** accrue, and Szoboszlai is being bought by the market
specifically for *"set-piece duties, defensive contribution potential and an attractive
price"*. The role change is a shift in *how* he scores, not a cut to whether he does. Hold.

### Horizon: 3 GWs instead of 8? — tested, and refused

*Owner question, 2026-08-19: "what if we look at the start for 3 GW instead of 8, since the
beginning of the season needs more power?" Worked in `notebooks/gw01_horizon_sensitivity.py`,
which decomposes each committed 8-GW total into a per-gameweek series (honouring each
overlay's `duration_gws`) and re-optimises the XI under eight different objectives.*

**The premise is half-right, and the half that's right is already priced.** The 0.85 decay
**already puts 53% of the 8-GW objective in GW1–3 and 66% in GW1–4**. This is not a squad
built for GW8.

| Objective | Shape | Captain | Matches our XI | Differences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 GW, decay 0.85 *(current)* | 3-5-2 | B.Fernandes | 8/11 | in B.Fern, João Pedro, Thiago · out Haaland, CL, Schade |
| 8 GW, decay 0.80 / 0.75 / 0.70 | 3-5-2 | B.Fernandes | 8/11 | *identical to the row above* |
| **5 GW, decay 0.85** | **3-5-2** | **Haaland** | **11/11** | **identical to ours** |
| 3 GW, decay 0.85 | 3-4-3 | Haaland | 9/11 | in Kelleher, Thiago · out Raya, Szoboszlai |
| 3 GW, flat | 3-4-3 | Haaland | 9/11 | *as above* |
| 1 GW only | 3-4-3 | Haaland | 9/11 | in Hume, João Pedro · out Szoboszlai, Thiaw |

Four things follow, and the third is the reason for the answer.

1. **Front-loading via the decay is a no-op.** Tightening 0.85 → 0.70 does not move a single
   player. The horizon debate is really a truncation debate.
2. **Our squad is already a 5-GW-optimal squad — 11/11, Haaland captain.** That is not a
   coincidence: the *pure* 8-GW solve wants B.Fernandes and João Pedro and ejects Haaland,
   and the manager overlay refused exactly that (the five-variant test above, V4). **The
   overlay has already delivered most of what a shorter horizon would deliver**, by hand and
   with written reasons. The owner's instinct and this squad are pointing the same way.
3. **A 3-GW objective would double down on the build's one known error.** The eight
   duration-3 World Cup fades — Rice 0.52, Saka 0.50, Guéhi 0.50, O'Reilly 0.38, Enzo, Rogers,
   Watkins, Anderson — are suppressed across **38% of an 8-GW window but 100% of a 3-GW one**.
   Truncating to three gameweeks roughly **2.6× the weight of exactly the overlay calls the
   Community Shield refuted**. The shorter horizon does not just fail to help; it mechanically
   amplifies the mistake we have already logged.
4. **The change it does suggest is not worth making.** Raya → Kelleher (freeing £1.0m) funding
   Szoboszlai → Thiago nets about **+0.2 points over the three gameweeks** — and it sells
   **Raya**, the single most news-validated pick in the squad (Golden Glove, Community Shield
   clean sheet, the softest fixture on the opening slate). Trading a confirmed certainty for
   two tenths of a point on a reconstructed model is the churn this project exists to refuse.

**Robustness.** The fitted fixture sensitivity is small — ~4% per FDR point (rms 0.24 against
the committed GW1 column) — and FDR is only a proxy for the Dixon-Coles expected goals the
model actually uses, so the sweep matters more than the point estimate. Raising it to 8%, 16%,
28% and 49% per point moves the 3-GW XI **further toward our squad, not away**: Haaland,
Calvert-Lewin and Schade come in, B.Fernandes and João Pedro go out. A stronger fixture effect
strengthens the squad we have.

**Verdict: keep the 8-GW horizon and the committed squad.** If a formal horizon change is ever
wanted, **5 gameweeks is the defensible number** — long enough to respect that squad membership
is sticky at one free transfer a week, short enough to stop weighting the noisiest quarter of
the projection — and it endorses this XI exactly.
