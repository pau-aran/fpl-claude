## Manager overlay (the human call, outside the algorithm)

**The thesis in one line: the 2026 World Cup ended 19 July and the market has not priced
it, so we fade the tournament's deep runners and buy the clubs that were home early.**

Mandated post-tournament rest runs to ~10–12 August. Roughly forty FPL-relevant players
reach this deadline with about ten days of club training and **no FPL flag of any kind** —
the risk is completely invisible in the FPL UI, which is where our edge has to come from
(Moneyball rule 2: minutes are the market inefficiency). It falls hardest on **Arsenal**
(Raya, Rice, Saka, Eze, Madueke, Zubimendi, Merino, with Saliba injured in the semi-final
and out 4–5 months) and **Man City** (Guéhi, O'Reilly, E. Anderson, Cherki, Trafford, with
Rodri post-surgery) — the two most-templated clubs in the game. **Liverpool are the least
damaged big club** and simultaneously own the best GW1–8 fixture run in the league.

That is the whole build. Fifty-six hand-written minutes overlays implement it
(`decisions/overlays/gw01.json`), each with a written reason and a duration. The durations
matter as much as the shares: the World Cup fatigue calls are **duration-3**, not season
judgements — these are good players who should be fit by GW4, and pricing a transient as
permanent is the overlay-horizon defect that drove three phantom sells in four backtest
weeks last season.

**What we deliberately do NOT own, and why:**

- **B.Fernandes (£12.0m, 47.9% owned) — the biggest call in the memo, and it is a fade.**
  Our own model rates him 2nd in the entire game (25.23 over the horizon) and he is fit,
  penalty-taker, set-piece hub, and has the best opening pair of fixtures in the league.
  This is *not* a doubt about the player. It is opportunity cost: at £12.0m he returns
  **2.10 pts/£m against Mbeumo's 2.72**, and we tested the substitution rather than
  asserting it. Locking him in costs ~0.8–1.0 objective points and, in the variant where he
  is locked alongside Raya and Gabriel, **forces Haaland out of the squad entirely**. The
  EO risk is real and is being taken knowingly — but it is blunted, because **we own
  Mbeumo, his team-mate on the identical HUL(A) + IPS(H) opening**, so the weeks Bruno
  hauls are largely the weeks our Man Utd asset returns too. Moneyball rule 1 applies
  literally here. **Revisit at T-48h and at GW2–3** — this is the line likeliest to change.
- **Rogers (£7.5m, 34.8% owned)** — faded on three independent counts: England
  semi-finalist, a £117m signing who joined Chelsea four days before our snapshot (so no
  pre-season and no system familiarity under a new manager), and he takes **no Chelsea set
  pieces** having been Villa's dead-ball man. Third-most-owned midfielder in the game.
- **Guéhi (25.3%), O'Reilly (24.8%), Porro (23.9%), Rice (22.5%), Konsa (18.4%)** — the
  template's cheap and mid-price core is disproportionately made of England and Spain
  semi-finalists and finalists. We own none of them for GW1.
- **Dubravka (£4.0m, 24.5% owned)** — our raw model ranked him **#1 in the entire game by
  points per million**, purely because it carried 35 starts he made for *Newcastle* into a
  Tottenham shirt he joined on 1 July, aged 37, behind two other keepers. That artifact is
  now fixed in the model and overridden in the overlay. 39.3% of the game's goalkeeper
  exposure is riding on an unresolved three-way battle; we took the knowable option
  instead.
- **All Bournemouth assets** — Truffert is genuinely one of the best value defenders in
  the game and our optimizer wanted him and Tavernier in the XI. Banned anyway:
  Bournemouth have the **worst opening run in the league** (summed FDR 28 over eight, GW1
  is Man City away) and the standing rule is *buy runs of fixtures, and never enter into
  the target's worst fixture*. He is a GW6+ watch, and with prices frozen we lose nothing
  by waiting.
- **All promoted-club goalkeepers** — all three situations are unresolved (Coventry's
  target is unsigned, Hull are chasing two more, Ipswich's £4.0m Palmer lost the shirt in
  October 2025).
- **Lacroix (12.9% owned)** — banned: Chelsea have agreed ~£52m, terms settled, medical
  booked. 12.9% of the game is holding an asset about to be repriced at a different club.

**What we own that the crowd does not, and why:**

- **Raya (£6.0m)** — the single most valuable correction in the overlay. He is a World Cup
  *champion* with Spain who played **zero minutes** in the entire tournament (Unai Simón
  started every game): a champion by medal, a spectator by workload. The market's
  "finalist ⇒ fatigued" heuristic is a false positive on exactly one player, and he is
  Arsenal's nailed No.1 behind the **softest opening fixture on the slate** (Coventry at
  home, FDR 2 v 5 — the biggest gap in GW1). We raised him against a club-wide Arsenal fade.
- **The Liverpool double, Wirtz (12.2%) + Szoboszlai (40.9%)** — best fixture run in the
  league, least World-Cup damage, both confirmed back in training after round-of-32 exits.
- **Schade (£6.0m, 2.3%)** — Germany out at the round of 32, fully rested, and our model's
  best value in the £6.0m midfield bracket.
- **O'Shea (£4.0m, 1.2%)** over **Diop (£4.0m, 21.4%)** — the same price for the Ipswich
  defender who played *every minute* of the promotion season as captain and centre of the
  back three, against one with 812 minutes to his name. Our model is 96% blind on Ipswich,
  so this is entirely research-derived.

**Positional-duel read** (standing owner directive; named so the review can grade it).
The named bet is **Mbeumo away at Hull (FDR 2)** — a fit, sharp, pre-season-complete
winger against a newly promoted defence, while the rest of the league's premium attackers
are ten days off a plane. Secondary: **Haaland at home to Bournemouth**, the softest
opening fixture available to any premium striker, against a side with the worst opening
run in the division. *Per the standing caution, neither named duel is a pure clean-sheet
bet — those went 0-for-2 last season and now require odds-grade support before naming.*

**Where we agree with the crowd, and why that is fine.** Haaland at 73.7% is not a
consensus-follow, it is our own model's top projection by a 4.1-point margin over the next
name, and he is the captaincy anchor. Not owning him would be a 74%-of-the-field bet
against our own numbers. Captaincy follows the standing rule — highest projection unless
news says otherwise — with no recency override.

## How the squad was chosen (five variants, tested not asserted)

The optimizer is a candidate generator, not an oracle. Five constrained solves, all on
the same overlay-adjusted projections:

| Variant | B.Fern | Haaland | Raya | Gabriel | 8-GW objective | GW1 XI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 raw (Bournemouth allowed) | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **249.00** | 50.20 |
| V2 | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | 246.13 | **51.65** |
| V3 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | 245.96 | 50.78 |
| V4 | ✓ | **✗** | ✓ | ✓ | 244.22 | 50.74 |
| **V5 → final** (+ bench refinement) | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 246.94 | 51.41 |

V1 scores highest but starts **two Bournemouth players**, which the fixture rule forbids;
banning them costs ~2.05 objective points and is the price of the discipline. V4 is the
warning: locking three premiums silently ejects Haaland. V2 posts the best single GW1
number, but it buys that by dropping **both** Raya and Gabriel — i.e. it spends the
Arsenal fixture edge and the freshest-keeper read to fund one £12.0m midfielder, and it is
the weakest of the five over the horizon that actually decides squad membership.

The spread across all five is **under 2%**, which is well inside the model's own error
bars given how many rows are flagged `low_sample` at a season rollover — so the choice was
made on research conviction and risk, not on the third decimal place.

## Standing plan — the transfer path (GW1 → GW4)

Written now so later hits can be checked against a plan rather than an impulse.

- **GW1 → GW2 is the Liverpool build.** Liverpool's GW1 is **Newcastle away (FDR 4), their
  hardest of the opening four**; from GW2 it is NFO(H) 3, IPS(A) 2, FUL(H) 2. We enter the
  run one gameweek early but deliberately *not* into its worst fixture — the exact rule
  that paid at GW9/GW10 last season. **Kerkez (£5.5m, 6.6% owned)** is the queued target:
  Iraola's Bournemouth full-backs were the most advanced in the league and he inherits the
  best fixture run in it. Frimpong (3.0%) is the same thesis on the right.
- **Man Utd's opening pair expires after GW2.** Their GW3–4 turns (EVE away, MCI home), so
  Mbeumo is a GW1–2 asset to reassess, not a season-long hold. Obi and Heaton are
  deliberately cheap, sellable Man Utd bodies — they exist partly so the 3-per-club cap does
  not block a B.Fernandes entry later.
- **The Arsenal question resolves itself on 16 August.** The Community Shield (Arsenal v
  Man City) is the only competitive fixture in which the late returners can actually be
  seen starting, and it lands **five days before the deadline**, after most of the market
  has drafted. Gabriel and Raya are held through it; Saka/Rice/Gyökeres entries are gated on it.
- **Bournemouth's run turns around GW6.** Truffert is on the watchlist, not the squad.
- **No hit is contemplated before GW2** and none passes the gate on current numbers.

## Risks, stated plainly

1. **The World Cup thesis is a forecast, not a fact.** If the deep returners are simply
   started anyway on 21 August, we have faded five of the ten most-owned players in the
   game for nothing. The Community Shield and the 17–20 August press conferences are the
   check, and there is time to reverse every line.
2. **No B.Fernandes.** The single largest ownership exposure we carry. Mitigated by
   Mbeumo, not eliminated.
3. **Four of fifteen are minutes blind spots** (Obi, O'Shea, van Ewijk, Heaton) — all four
   are bench pieces, and all four are cheap enablers at clubs our model is 93–96% blind on.
   None of them starts. This is the honest cost of a season-opening build.
4. **The defence is thin and cheap** behind Gabriel: Tarkowski and Thiaw at £6.0m/£5.0m,
   with two promoted-club defenders as cover. Thiaw's GW1 is Liverpool at home (FDR 4).
5. **A 3-5-2 with two forwards** means an early Haaland or Calvert-Lewin absence pulls in
   Obi, a £4.5m unknown.
6. **Model caveat carried forward, deliberately unfixed**: injury flags are still priced as
   8-GW absences rather than scoped durations. This is mitigated per-player in the overlay —
   every flagged player in this squad's consideration set carries an explicit
   `duration_gws` — and guessing a default duration from a flag would invent information we
   do not have. Documented as D1 in `research/2026-27/model-fixes.md`.

   *The Ipswich club-name mismatch previously listed here is **fixed** (D6). Worth recording
   why it took two attempts: correcting the name alone would have been worse than the bug,
   because `covers()` counted matches without checking their age — Ipswich would then have
   been Dixon-Coles rated on the very relegation season that sent them down. The first fix
   attempt also broke Leeds by expanding a club name FPL leaves bare. Both are now pinned by
   tests in `tests/test_prior_grading.py`.*
