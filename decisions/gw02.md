## 1. Why roll — the case, and the case against

The bar for a free transfer is the A4 per-move edge floor: **0.5 decayed horizon points.**
One candidate clears it on the numbers, and is declined on a written rule.

### The candidate: Thiaw → De Cuyper

**Maxim De Cuyper (BHA, £4.6m)** was Gameweek 1's standout: **17 points** — goal, assist,
clean sheet, 2 bonus — off a game-high **13.9 expected FPL points** and joint-top for big
chances. He is not a bandwagon in the ordinary sense: he is an **FPL-classified defender
deployed on the left wing**, which is the exact positional arbitrage the GW1 review names as
our biggest blind spot (§2.8). ~250,000 managers have already bought him; he rose to £4.6m
on Wednesday, the first price change of the season.

He is affordable — Thiaw sells at £5.0m, so the swap *funds itself and returns £0.4m*.
Over GW3–4 the case is emphatic: **Brighton LEE(H) 2 + COV(A) 2 = 4, against Newcastle's
BOU(H) 3 + LEE(A) 3 = 6.**

### Why it is declined this week

**Brighton's GW2 is Chelsea away — FDR 4, his joint-worst fixture of the next four.**
Our standing rule, written in the GW1 memo and applied there to ban Truffert, is:
*buy runs of fixtures, and never enter into the target's worst fixture.*

We wrote that rule three weeks ago, banned an asset the optimizer wanted in order to honour
it, and it graded **PASS** in the GW1 review. Breaking it in GW2 for the week's most-bought
player would be the same failure the iteration-2 amendment caught and fixed — *the rule was
written and then not applied to the player the solver happened to pick* — except this time
the player is one the crowd picked. That is worse, not better.

**Three further reasons the roll is the stronger move:**

1. **2 FT at GW3 buys a paired move, and GW3 is when three things resolve at once:** the
   **1 September transfer deadline** (club-move risk on our assets is live and unverifiable
   today), **Man Utd's fixture turn** (IPS(H) is their last soft fixture until GW8 — EVE(A),
   MCI(H), FUL(A) follow), and **Brighton's run turning** (LEE H, COV A). One FT cannot take
   both the defensive upgrade and the Man Utd exit; two can.
2. **We are one gameweek into a season whose central thesis was just falsified.** The right
   response to being wrong about a forecast is to stop forecasting for a week and read, not
   to trade on the next forecast immediately.
3. **Nothing else in the squad clears the floor.** Semenyo (City, CRY away), Mbeumo (his best
   remaining fixture), Wirtz and Szoboszlai (the league's best GW2–5 run) are all holds on
   their own numbers. Calvert-Lewin is a £6.0m enabler doing an enabler's job.

### The cost of rolling, stated plainly

De Cuyper will very likely rise again before Friday and again before GW3 — call it
**£4.7–4.8m**, so **~£0.1–0.2m of team value**. That is the price of the discipline and it
is worth naming rather than hiding. Points beat team value in August; £0.2m buys nothing in
GW3 that £0.0m does not.

**If he rises past £4.9m by the GW3 deadline, revisit — at that point the funding maths, not
the fixture rule, becomes the binding constraint.**

## 2. Manager overlay — what changes after GW1

### 2.1 The World Cup minutes overlays are RETIRED, effective now

The GW1 build rested on fading the tournament's deep runners on **minutes** grounds. Every
named returner started: Guéhi 90', O'Reilly 79', E. Anderson 84' for City; Saka (scored),
Rice, White (assist) for Arsenal. Three of our named fades — **Guéhi, Saka, Rogers** — returned.

The overlays were written `duration_gws: 3`, so they still cover GW2 and GW3. **They are
retired here**, and nothing replaces them.

**But the underlying read was not wrong — it was filed under the wrong variable.** Maresca
said on the record that Haaland and the other returners "only returned around 10 days ago",
calling this stage *an extension of pre-season, with a little rust*. Haaland then generated
**0.85 xG — over a third of City's 2.36 — and scored none.**

> **The corrected form: post-tournament fatigue in 2026/27 is a finishing/sharpness effect,
> not a minutes effect.** It is a reason to be wary of *captaining* a rusty returner. It is
> not a reason to *own less of one*. This memo acts on the corrected version in exactly one
> place — §4, the captaincy — and nowhere else.

### 2.2 B.Fernandes — the fade is re-opened, as promised, and it holds

The GW1 memo committed to revisiting this "at T-48h and at GW2–3". Doing so now:

- He **blanked** (~2) in a 2-0 defeat at Hull. Mbeumo, the £8.0m same-fixture asset we hold
  instead, also returned ~2. **Identical output, £4.0m cheaper.**
- He is **51.9% owned** — the EO exposure is real and unchanged.
- **He is unaffordable regardless**: £12.0m in with £0.0m bank means selling Mbeumo (£8.0m)
  and finding £4.0m more. There is no single-FT route.
- Man Utd's GW3–5 is EVE(A), MCI(H), FUL(A). We do not want *more* Man Utd exposure into
  that; the plan is to reduce it.

**Fade holds.** It is now graded twice and has cost nothing.

### 2.3 The positional-classification pass — a new standing step

GW1's two biggest hauls were both players whose FPL position is cheaper than the role they
actually play: **De Cuyper** (defender, left wing, 17 pts) and **Guéhi** (defender, central
midfield, ~11 pts). We wrote 56 overlays about tournament fatigue and none about this.

From GW3 the scout pass runs a positional-classification sweep before every deadline. It is
already driving the queued move (§5). Two names to check against Friday's line-ups rather
than buy today: **Guéhi**, if Maresca keeps him in midfield — a defender-priced midfielder in
a title-race team is a structurally underpriced asset, and Palace away is a soft re-entry.

### 2.4 The named positional duel — **none named this week**

The GW1 duel (Mbeumo at Hull) failed because it priced the player and ignored the team.
The new constraint: *a named duel must clear a team-level check — never name a duel for an
attacker whose side is the fixture's underdog on the underlying.* Nothing in our GW2 XI
clears that bar cleanly enough to be worth naming, so nothing is named. Per the standing
caution, no pure clean-sheet duel is named either.

## 5. Chip: NONE. All 8 retained.

Wildcard and Free Hit both open at GW2 this season. Neither fires, and the wildcard deserves
an explicit refusal rather than silence:

- **Wildcard — the tempting one, and it is wrong.** The argument for it is real: the squad
  was built on a thesis GW1 falsified, so wildcard out of it. **The argument fails on the
  evidence.** The falsified thesis governed who we *did not buy*, not the soundness of the
  fifteen we hold — and those fifteen scored **~48 against a 45.09 base-XI projection**. The
  base XI beat its number. A squad that beats its projection is not broken, and burning the
  first-half wildcard in August — 17 gameweeks before its natural use in an October fixture
  swing or an injury crisis — to fix a squad that is working would be a panic move wearing a
  thesis for cover.
- **Free Hit** — no blank or double gameweek. Nothing to hit.
- **Triple Captain** — CRY(A) at FDR 3 is not a standout captain fixture. City's **COV(H)
  (FDR 2) in GW3** is strictly better, and a double gameweek is better still. Standing rule:
  TC on a standout fixture or a DGW. Not met.
- **Bench Boost** — our bench is van Ewijk, O'Shea, Obi (0 minutes in GW1, not even in the
  Man Utd matchday squad) and Heaton (third-choice keeper). Worth perhaps 5 points. Absurd.
